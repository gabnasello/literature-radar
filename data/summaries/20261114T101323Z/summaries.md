## Nonlinear Electro-Optic Visible Photonic Circuits for Solid-State Quantum Defects.

PMID: 42870537 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42870537/

**Summary**

This study investigates a monolithic thin-film lithium niobate platform integrating nonlinear frequency conversion and high-speed electro-optic switching for quantum defect manipulation. The approach combines periodically poled frequency conversion with GHz-bandwidth electro-opt. switching to generate milliwatt-scale green light with 42.2 dB extinction ratio. Results enable coherent spin control and time-resolved lifetime measurements of nitrogen-vacancy centers via nanosecond gating, demonstrating a scalable framework for quantum communication nodes.

**Key findings**

- Monolithic thin-film lithium niobate platform integrates nonlinear frequency conversion and GHz-bandwidth electro-optic switching
- Device delivers over 1 mW green-light power with 42.2 dB extinction ratio
- Enables coherent spin control and time-resolved lifetime measurements of nitrogen-vacancy centers

**Study type**

Monolithic thin-film lithium niobate platform with integrated nonlinear frequency conversion and electro-optic switching

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes a scalable platform for high-rate quantum communication nodes by unifying milliwatt-scale visible light generation with high-speed active manipulation of solid-state quantum defects.

## A pseudoenzyme sensor of trehalose-6-phosphate directly represses the metabolic integrator SnRK1 in plants.

**Key findings**

- T6P binding represses SnRK1 kinase activity to regulate metabolic pathways
- TPS-II loss activates stress responses and suppresses anabolic gene expression

**Study type**

Functional and chemical biology approaches

## A Universal Spin-Polarization-Engineered Antibonding-Orbital Modulation Paradigm for Accelerating Reaction Kinetics in Ultrahigh-Rate Sodium-Ion Batteries.

PMID: 42870538 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42870538/

**Summary**

This study addresses the kinetic limitations in conversion-type sodium-ion battery anodes by introducing a spin-polarization-engineered antibonding-orbital modulation approach. Magnetic doping induces spin-state transitions that enhance electron occupation in antibonding orbitals, strengthening metal-chalcogen bonds. The Mn-FeS2/CoS2 anode achieves ultrahigh-rate performance (300 mA h g-1 at 120 A g-1) and exceptional cycling stability (100% retention after 13,000 cycles).

**Key findings**

- Magnetic manganese doping triggers a spin-state transition from low-spin to high-spin configurations
- Enhanced electron occupation in antibonding orbitals strengthens metal-chalcogen bonds
- Mn-FeS2/CoS2 anode demonstrates ultrahigh-rate capability (300 mA h g-1 at 120 A g-1) and 100% retention over 13,000 cycles

**Study type**

Proof of concept with magnetic heteroatom doping

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes a universal strategy for accelerating reaction kinetics in conversion-type anodes by manipulating spin states to regulate bond strength, offering a pathway to overcome intrinsic kinetic barriers in sodium-ion batteries.

## Turning Nd-Fe-B Swarf Into High-Value Bulk Feedstocks via Sustainable Hydrogen Plasma: From Rare-Earth Oxides to Metals and Hydrides.

PMID: 42870553 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42870553/

**Summary**

This study investigates sustainable recycling of rare-earth permanent magnet (REPM) machining swarf using hydrogen plasma reduction (HPR). The approach involves tuning hydrogen content and treatment time to separate metallic iron and a Nd2O3-rich rare-earth fraction, enabling direct rare-earth hydride formation. The process achieves high-purity feedstock without acid use, reducing CO2 emissions by up to 70 kg CO2-eq per kg compared to conventional REPM production.

**Key findings**

- Hydrogen plasma reduction separates metallic iron and Nd2:O3-rich rare-earth fractions from REPM swarf
- The method enables direct rare-earth hydride formation in molten state
- The process reduces CO2 emissions by up to 70 kg CO2-eq per kg of REPM compared to conventional production

**Study type**

Hydrogen plasma reduction (HPR) process

**Scientific significance**

This work provides a scalable pathway for sustainable rare-earth recycling, significantly lowering the carbon footprint of permanent magnet production while enhancing resource efficiency without chemical reagents.

**Study type**

## Magnetic-Field-Annealing-Induced Short-Range Order in High-Entropy Alloys for Enhanced Electromagnetic Response.

PMID: 42872304 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42872304/

**Summary**

This study investigates magnetic-field-annealing (MFA) in high-entropy alloys to optimize microwave absorbers for 5G/6G applications. By applying MFA at 0.5 T to FeCoNiCr composites, researchers achieved a reflection loss minimum of -72.30 dB at .59 mm and a 4.40 GHz absorption bandwidth. **Key findings**

- MFA at 0.5 T in FeCoNiCr alloys achieved a reflection loss minimum of -72.30 dB at 1.59 mm
- Maximum effective absorption bandwidth of 4.40 GHz at room temperature, retaining 92.7% of bandwidth at 85°C
- Co-centered short-range order explains the electromagnetic response through atomic-scale mechanisms

**Study type**

Magnetic field annealing (MFA) of high-entropy alloys

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes magnetic-field-annealing as a strategy to engineer short-range order in high-entropy alloys, enhancing microwave absorption properties while maintaining thermal stability for next-generation communication technologies.
