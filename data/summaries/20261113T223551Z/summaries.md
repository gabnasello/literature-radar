## Achieving High Spatial Resolution Transmission Electron Microscopy Studies in Closed Liquid Cells.

PMID: 42885387 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42885387/

**Summary**

This review examines strategies to enhance spatial resolution in liquid cell transmission electron microscopy (LCTEM) by addressing electron scattering from membranes and liquid layers. It evaluates MEMS-based designs, ultrathin windows, and liquid layer thickness optimization to achieve higher resolution imaging while maintaining dynamic process observation in liquid environments.

**Key findings**

- Reducing liquid and membrane thicknesses minimizes electron scattering
- Optimizing MEMS architectures improves imaging quality
- Grid-based liquid cells enable high-resolution dynamic observations

**Scientific significance**

This work advances LCTEM by systematically addressing resolution limitations through material and design innovations, enabling atomic-scale imaging of liquid-phase processes with enhanced structural and dynamic resolution.

## An injectable ROS-responsive natural drug self-delivery hydrogel relieves neuropathic pain by remodeling DRG macrophage-mediated neuroinflammation.

PMID: 42945945 | Journal: Biomaterials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42945945/

**Summary**

This study investigated an injectable ROS-responsive hydrogel for neuropathic pain relief by targeting DRG macrophage-mediated neuroinflammation. Using glycyrrhizic acid (GA) as a PI3K-targeting agent, the hydrogel was engineered to release GA in response to inflammation-associated ROS. In rats with neuropathic pain, peri-DRG administration reduced hypersensitivity, improved function, and decreased macrophage-driven neuroinflammation, demonstrating localized efficacy in pain modulation.

**Key findings**

- GPG hydrogel releases GA in response to ROS, suppressing PI3K-Akt signaling in DRG macrophages
- Peri-DRG administration alleviated mechanical and thermal hypersensitivity in neuropathic pain models
- The hydrogel reduced DRG macrophage-mediated neuroinflammation and spinal microglial activation

**Study type**

In vivo rat model of neuropathic pain

**Scientific significance**

This ROS-triggered natural drug delivery system provides a targeted mechanism to remodel DRG neuroinflammation, offering a minimally invasive approach for sustained neuropathic pain relief without systemic side effects.

## Polyacrylate-Based Dielectric Elastomer Fiber Actuators for High-Performance Artificial Muscles.

PMID: 42885403 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42885403/

**Summary**

This study developed polyacrylate-based dielectric elastomer fiber actuators (PA-DEFAs) using continuous coaxial wet spinning with in situ UV curing. The approach enabled scalable fabrication while maintaining high electromechanical performance, yielding actuators with up to 39% axial strain, 54.8 J kg-1 energy density, and 443 W kg- 1 peak power density. The fibers support modular integration into soft robotic systems.

**Key findings**

- Continuous fabrication of PA-DEFAs via coaxial wet spinning and in situ UV curing
- Maximum prestretch-free axial strain of ∼39% and energy density of 54.8 J kg-1
- Fibers enable modular integration into soft robotic systems like robotic arms and underwater manta-rays

**Study type**

Continuous fabrication strategy with coaxial wet spinning and in situ UV curing

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes a scalable manufacturing platform for high-performance dielectric elastomer actuators, bridging the gap between continuous production and electromechanical performance for artificial muscles.

- Critical transition from charge-transfer-dominated to balanced localized-excitation/charge-transfer hybrid excited states
- Oxygen-independent Type-I photodynamic activity
- 87..4% photothermal conversion efficiency under 0.33 W/cm² 808 nm irradiation

**Study type**

Progressive donor-acceptor intensification strategy

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes a paradigm for excited-state composition engineering in NIR-II photosensitizers, enabling phototheranostic applications under clinically relevant low-power laser conditions without reliance on hypoxic environments or high-intensity irradiation.

## Innate sensor responses set a supra-unitary threshold for viral replication.

PMID: 42956738 | Journal: Cell
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42956738/

**Summary**

The study investigates how cells resist viral infection via innate immune sensors, identifying a supra-unitary viral genome threshold (VITREP) for replication. Using single-cell analysis and mathematical modeling, it demonstrates that viral replication requires more than one viral genome, with negative feedback regulators (NFR) suppressing immune responses to enable replication. This threshold varies by virus and cell type, independent of viral protein synthesis.

**Key findings**

- Viral replication requires a supra-unitary viral genome input (VITREP), exceeding one genome
- Negative feedback regulators (NFR) like DAPK1, RIOK3, and PPP2R2A suppress innate immune responses to allow replication
- The VITREP threshold varies across viruses and cell types, independent of viral protein synthesis

**Study type**

Single-cell analysis and mathematical modeling

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes a fundamental mechanism where supra-unitary viral genome thresholds govern viral infectivity and tissue tropism through innate immune regulation.

**Key findings**

- Orb2 amyloid transitions from translation-promoting states in young Drosophila to destabilization-resistant states in aging flies
- Aging shifts Orb2 amyloid ensemble toward reduced translational activity, coinciding with memory impairment
- Manipulating memory-related chaperones can shift Orb2 toward youthful states and improve memory in old flies

**Study type**

Cryogenic electron microscopy

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes an in vivo trajectory of amyloid structural evolution linked to synaptic translation and memory decline, providing a model for understanding age-related amyloid dynamics in living organisms.

## Piezoelectric electroporation potentiates high-frequency irreversible electroporation for enhanced prostate cancer eradication.

PMID: 42962342 | Journal: Biomaterials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42962342/

**Summary**

This study investigated whether piezoelectric electroporation could enhance high-frequency irreversible electroporation (H-FIRE) for prostate cancer treatment by targeting residual tumor cells. Using a piezoelectric nanomaterial (BOIS), the researchers exploited H-FIRE-induced membrane permeability to deliver BOIS into residual cells. Upon ultrasound activation, BOIS generated an electric field and reactive oxygen species (ROS), converting reversible injury to irreversible cell death. The approach significantly improved eradication of residual tumor cells without additional external energy input.

**Key findings**

- H-FIRE-induced membrane permeability enabled intracellular delivery of piezoelectric nanomaterial BOIS into residual prostate cancer cells
- Ultrasound-activated BOIS generated an electric field and ROS, causing simultaneous membrane and nuclear damage
- This dual mechanism converted reversibly injured residual cells into irreversible lethal damage, enhancing tumor eradication

**Study type**

In vitro and in vivo models

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes a self-reinforcing therapeutic loop where focal ablation (H-FIRE) delivers nanomaterials to induce irreversible cell death in residual tumor cells, offering a novel strategy for complete tumor eradication post-ablation.

**Summary**

This study investigates how fatty acid desaturation influences cell fate decisions between senescence and ferroptosis. Using cellular and in vivo models, researchers found that senescent cells exhibit reduced dihomo-gamma-linolenic acid (DGLA) levels, while exogenous DGLA supplementation induces ferroptosis. **Key findings**

- Senescent cells show reduced levels of dihomo-gamma-linolenic acid (DGLA, 20:3ω-6)
- Exogenous DGLA supplementation triggers ferroptosis in cells
- Oral DGLA reduces senescence markers in aged mice and improves functional outcomes

**Study type**

In vivo and cellular models

**Scientific significance**

The study identifies a specific fatty acid metabolic switch that governs the decision between senescence and ferroptosis, providing a mechanistic link between lipid metabolism and cellular aging pathways.

## Biomaterial thrombosis-on-a-chip: Real-time visualisation of biomaterial thrombosis at clinical heparin levels.

PMID: 42967418 | Journal: Biomaterials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42967418/

**Summary**

This study developed a biomaterial thrombosis-on-a-chip model using human whole blood to evaluate acute thrombogenicity of medical devices under clinical heparin levels (0.5-5 U/mL) and shear rates (50-2000 s-1). It revealed shear-dependent fibrin events, differential fibrin responses across materials, and heparin concentration effects on fibrin aggregation without altering platelet adhesion dynamics.

**Key findings**

- Shear-dependent decrease in fibrin events on hydrophilic glass but not hydrophobic silicone
- Albumin pre-coating abolished fibrin on silicone but not glass
- Lower heparin levels (0.5 U/mL) induced more and larger fibrin aggregates compared to higher levels (5 U/mL)

**Study type**

In vitro thrombosis-on-a-chip model

**Scientific significance**

This model provides real-time visualization of acute biomaterial thrombosis mechanisms under clinically relevant conditions, enabling rapid assessment of anti-thrombotic material efficacy without reliance on traditional in vivo or long-term in vitro methods.

## Convergent genomic evolution of human gut bacteria along an industrialization gradient.

PMID: 42971204 | Journal: Cell
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42971204/

**Summary**

This study investigates genomic adaptation differences between gut bacteria in industrialized versus non-industrialized human populations. Using isolate genomes across diverse geographies and host lifestyles, it found industrialized hosts' gut bacteria exhibit expanded proteomes and increased pangenome fluidity due to recent horizontal gene transfer. Convergent adaptations in stress response, cell-envelope remodeling, and central metabolism were observed, indicating industrialization drives convergent genome evolution in gut commensals.

**Key findings**

- Industrialized human hosts' gut bacteria show expanded proteome size compared to non-industrialized populations
- Strains from industrialized hosts exhibit greater pangenome fluidity driven by recent horizontal gene transfer events
- Convergent adaptations in stress response, cell as well as central metabolism pathways were identified across industrialized populations

**Study type**

Genomic analysis of gut bacterial isolates across industrialized and non-industrialized human populations

**Scientific significance**

The study reveals industrialization drives convergent genome evolution in gut commensals, providing insights into microbial adaptation mechanisms to rapidly changing human environments.
