## Enzyme-embedded polyesters for accelerated biodegradation in the deep sea.

PMID: 42907514 | Journal: Nature
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42907514/

**Summary**

This study investigates enhancing the biodegradation of compostable polyesters in deep-sea environments by embedding enzymes within them. The researchers tested enzyme-embedded polyesters (PBS, PBSA, PCL, PBAT) at depths up to 5,503 meters. Results showed near-complete degradation at 757 meters within five months and moderate degradation at extreme depths. The approach maintains mechanical properties while enabling rapid marine breakdown.

**Key findings**

- Enzyme-embedded polyesters degraded near-completely at 757 m deep-sea depth within five months
- Moderate biodegradation occurred at extreme depths of 5,503 m
- Mechanical properties remained comparable to enzyme-free polyesters

**Scientific significance**

This strategy provides a scalable method for accelerating marine biodegradation of compostable plastics, addressing a critical gap in deep-sea plastic pollution mitigation.

## Genomic erosion across avifauna in a biodiversity hotspot sounds a conservation alarm.

PMID: 42956122 | Journal: Science
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42956122/

**Summary**

This study quantified genetic diversity loss in 62 songbird species from China's Gaol. Mountains biodiversity hotspot by comparing historical and modern genomes. It found genomic erosion averaging 43-49%, disproportionately affecting common species. The loss is driven by habitat fragmentation, exceeds conservation thresholds, and persists despite species being listed as 'Least Concern' on the IUCN Red List, highlighting a critical gap in extinction risk monitoring.

**Key findings**

- Genomic erosion averaged 43-49% across 62 songbird species in a Chinese biodiversity hotspot
- Common species were disproportionately affected by genetic diversity loss
- The erosion exceeds conservation policy thresholds and persists despite IUCN 'Least Concern' listings

**Scientific significance**

The study demonstrates that genetic diversity loss in songbirds can occur rapidly in biodiversity hotspots, challenging current conservation metrics and underscoring the need for genomic assessments in extinction risk monitoring.

## Beyond State of Charge: Electrochemical States and Trajectory Design in Organic Mixed Conductors.

PMID: 42920199 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42920199/

**Summary**

This review establishes a state-centered framework for organic mixed ionic-electronic conductors (OMIECs), moving beyond scalar descriptors like voltage or charge. It introduces a minimal sufficient state basis to distinguish essential coordinates, while waveform history and spatial heterogeneity determine when lumped descriptions are valid. The framework enables reconstructing hidden states and designing multidimensional electrochemical trajectories for applications such as electrochromics, OECTs, and energy storage devices.

**Key findings**

- A minimal sufficient state basis identifies essential coordinates for OMIECs
- Waveform history and spatial heterogeneity define validity of lumped state descriptions
- The framework enables reconstruction of hidden states and trajectory design for electrochemical applications

**Scientific significance**

This framework provides a unified approach to model and design electrochemical states in OMIECs, advancing understanding of how molecular structure, device architecture, and driving protocols influence observable, reversible, and durable electrochemical trajectories.

## Monolithically and in situ integrated PbS infrared imagers.

PMID: 42951959 | Journal: Nature
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42951959/

**Summary**

This study investigates monolithic in situ integration of lead sulfide (PbS) infrared detectors directly onto readout integrated circuits (ROICs) using a room-temperature aqueous process. By controlling Pb-OH complexation and minimizing environmental interference, the researchers achieved quasi-single-crystalline PbS films with high electronic properties and pixel-level uniformity. The fabricated 640×512-pixel array demonstrated record-specific detectivity at 2.5 µm, enabling clear infrared imaging and flexible ROIC integration.

**Key findings**

- Quasi-single-crystalline PbS films with carrier density ~2×10^15 cm^-3 and mobility 124 cm^2 V^-1 s^-1
- Record specific detectivity of 4.4×10^11 Jones at 2.5 µm, exceeding commercial InGaAs detectors
- 640×512-pixel focal-plane array capturing clear infrared images and direct integration onto flexible ROICs

**Scientific significance**

This solution-based monolithic integration method provides a scalable pathway for high-performance infrared detectors, advancing optoelectronic sensor technology without traditional bonding processes.
