## Direct imaging elucidates ionic memory in two-dimensional nanochannels.

PMID: 42946763 | Journal: Nature Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42946763/

**Summary**

This study investigates the microscopic origin of ionic memory in two-dimensional nanochannels using operando interferometric imaging and electrokinetic measurements. It directly visualizes voltage-induced blistering of confining walls as the cause of memristive hysteresis, identifying unidirectional blisters from electrostatic forces and bidirectional blusing from osmotic pressure. The findings provide a mechanistic framework for device behavior and variability.

**Key findings**

- Direct visualization of voltage-induced blistering in 2D nanochannels as the origin of memristive hysteresis
- Identification of unidirectional blisters driven by electrostatic forces on surface charges
- Bidirectional blisters resulting from osmotic pressure due to concentration polarization

**Study type**

Operando interferometric imaging with electrokinetic measurements

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes electromechanical coupling as a direct mechanism for ionic memory in 2D nanochannels, advancing understanding of nanofluidic memristors and enabling more precise design of ionic-based computing systems.

## Late Holocene reexpansion of the Laurentide Ice Sheet.

PMID: 42912134 | Journal: Science
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42912134/

**Summary**

This study investigates the Late Holocene reexpansion of the Laurentide Ice Sheet (LIS) using ice core and geological evidence. It demonstrates that the LIS reexpanded ~2000 years ago after a minimum configuration established ~3.6 thousand years ago, marking the start of the next glaciation. The ice sheet reached maximum extent by ~1960 CE, subsequently retreating due to anthropogenic warming, ending the glaciation.

**Key findings**

- The LIS achieved its minimum interglial dimensions ~3.6 thousand years ago
- Reexpansion occurred ~2000 years ago, initiating the next glaciation
- Maximum LIS extent reached ~1960 CE before anthropogenic-driven retreat

**Scientific significance**

The findings highlight how anthropogenic climate change can terminate glacial cycles by accelerating ice sheet retreat, providing evidence for greenhouse gas forcing's role in overriding natural climate cycles.
