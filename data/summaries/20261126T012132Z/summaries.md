## Rate Capability of Lithium-Rich Manganese-Based Oxide Cathodes: Charge-Discharge Kinetics, Multiscale Bottlenecks, and Dynamic Rate-Determining Steps.

PMID: 43004143 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43004143/

**Summary**

This review investigates rate capability limitations in lithium-rich manganese oxide (LRMO) cathodes, identifying three hierarchical kinetic bottlenecks: direct charge-transfer impediments, sluggish anionic redox kinetics, and degradation cascades. It demonstrates that the rate-determining step shifts from electron-transfer to ion-diffusion dominance, influenced by exchange and limiting current densities. The study emphasizes charging kinetics as more critical for fast charging than discharging and highlights methodological inconsistencies in cross-study C-rate comparisons that hinder performance evaluation. The findings provide a theoretical framework for designing high-rate LRMO cathodes.

**Key findings**

- The rate-determining step shifts from electron-transfer to ion-diffusion dominance in LRMO cathodes
- Charging kinetics impose a more severe bottleneck for fast charging than discharging
- Methodological inconsistencies in C-rate comparisons due to electrode loading and testing variations limit cross-study performance evaluation

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes a hierarchical framework for understanding rate-limiting mechanisms in LRMO cathodes, enabling targeted design strategies to enhance high-rate performance for next-generation battery applications.

## Early-Life Farm Exposure and Food Allergy.

PMID: 43067475 | Journal: Annals of allergy, asthma & immunology : official publication of the American College of Allergy, Asthma, & Immunology
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43067475/

**Summary**

This review examines the relationship between early-life farm exposure and food allergy, focusing on prenatal and childhood exposures. It synthesizes evidence that farm environments may promote immune tolerance through livestock contact, farm dust, microbial exposure, and diet. Protective effects are hypothesized to involve gut microbiome development, reduced inflammation, and regulatory T-cell responses. However, direct evidence for food allergy prevention remains limited compared to asthma, highlighting the need for mechanistic studies to identify actionable pathways for intervention.

**Key findings**

- Early farm exposures correlate with reduced food allergy risk through multiple environmental factors
- Protective mechanisms likely involve gut microbiome maturation and immune regulation
- Direct evidence linking farm exposure to clinically confirmed food allergy is limited compared to asthma

**Scientific significance**

This review identifies critical pathways for understanding how early environmental exposures may prevent food allergy, emphasizing the need for mechanistic research to develop targeted interventions.

- Broadband photoresponsivity of 103 A/W across 1060-1470 nm with 96% lower bias voltage versus standalone PTF
- Exceptional long-term stability with maintained performance after six months of ambient storage

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes mixed-dimensional heterostructures as a viable pathway to high-performance, stable SWIR photodetectors through synergistic design principles, addressing critical challenges in carrier collection and interface engineering.
