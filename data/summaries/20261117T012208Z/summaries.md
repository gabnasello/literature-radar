## Imaging electron-hole asymmetry in the quantum melting of generalized Wigner crystals.

PMID: 42912157 | Journal: Science
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42912157/

**Summary**

This study used scanning tunneling microscopy to investigate quantum melting transitions in generalized Wigner crystals (GWCs) and Mott insulators within twisted molybdenum diselenide bilayers. It revealed electron-hole asymmetry during GWC melting: electron-doped systems formed delocalized liquid-like states, while hole-doped systems exhibited interaction-driven disorder. The asymmetry stems from broken particle-hole symmetry in the moiré superlattice, creating distinct electron and hole Fermi pockets with different momentum geometries.

**Key findings**

- Electron-doped GWCs melted into delocalized liquid-like states
- Hole-doped GWCs formed interaction-driven disordered states
- Broken particle-hole symmetry in the moiré superlattice generated distinct electron and hole Fermi pockets with different momentum geometries

**Scientific significance**

The study provides direct visualization of emergent phases during quantum melting transitions in generalized Wigner crystals, revealing electron-hole asymmetry as a consequence of broken particle-hole symmetry in moiré superlattices.

## Drought and disease trigger novel interactions among top predators.

PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42907515/

**Summary**

This study investigates how drought and disease alter predator interactions among top vertebrates. Using field observations, researchers found that a decline in shared prey (rabbits) due to disease triggered lynx to prey on kites exclusively during droughts. This behavior reduced kite reproductive success and caused dispersal, while increasing small owl populations. The findings highlight diet flexibility and mobility as critical resilience factors under climatic stress.

**Key findings**

- Drought and disease caused lynx to prey on k. kites after rabbit decline
- Lynx predation reduced kite reproductive success and induced dispersal
- Increased small owl abundance followed raptor population decline

**Scientific significance**

## Programmable design of functional proteins from natural language.

PMID: 42907516 | Journal: Nature
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42907516/

**Summary**

This study investigates using natural language to programmatically design functional proteins. Pinal, a 16-billion-parameter foundation model trained on 1.7 billion protein-text pairs, translates functional descriptions into candidate protein sequences and structures. Computational evaluations showed high foldability, functional alignment, and diversity. Experimentally, Pinal designed four proteins with catalytic activity, including a 1.7-fold increase in product titre for a CO2 fixation pathway H-protein compared to native variants.

**Key findings**

- Pinal designs functional proteins directly from natural-language descriptions without manual constraint reformulation
- Generated candidates demonstrated high predicted foldability and functional alignment in computational tests
- Experimentally validated catalytic activity for four designed proteins, including a 1.7-fold improvement in a CO2 fixation pathway

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes natural language as a viable high-level interface for programmable protein design, reducing reliance on manual structural or sequence constraints while enabling functional validation without iterative optimization.

By varying Cl-/Br- ratios and quantifying inter. amorphicity, the research identifies a volcano-shaped relationship where intermediate halide compositions optimize ionic transport through moderately disordered grain boundaries. **Key findings**

- Intermediate Cl-/Br- ratios maximize ionic transport by balancing grain boundary amorphicity
- Cl-enriched grain boundaries exhibit excessive order, while Br-enriched regions show excessive disorder
- Anion-engineered grain boundaries reduce interfacial resistance through controlled halide segregation

**Scientific significance**

## Synergistic Interaction Engineering Enables Homogeneous Mixed-Cation Perovskite Single Crystals for High-Performance X-Ray Detection.

PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42920386/

**Key findings**

- Acetic acid enables synergistic interaction engineering to balance crystallization rates of formamidinium and methylammonium phases
- Elimination of compositional gradients across single crystals through kinetic regulation
- X-ray detectors achieved sensitivity of 3.54 × 10^6 µC Gy-1 cm-2 and detection limit of 2.65 nGy s-1

**Scientific significance**

This kinetic-regulation strategy provides a general method for producing homogeneous mixed-cation perovskite single crystals, advancing x-ray detector performance without compositional inhomogeneity.
