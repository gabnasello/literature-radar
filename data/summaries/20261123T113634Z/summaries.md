## Aging drives distinct chromatin signatures of CD8 T cell dysfunction and the emergence of a unique CD8 Treg cell population.

PMID: 43063303 | Journal: Science immunology
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43063303/

**Summary**

This study investigated age-related changes in CD8 T cell function using chromatin accessibility mapping in young and aged mice. It identified three distinct chromatin signatures associated with dysfunction: reduced stemness gene accessibility, decreased restraint gene accessibility in exhausted memory cells, and increased terminal inhibitory gene accessibility in senescent and exhausted subsets. The research also revealed a novel CD8 regulatory T cell population emerging with aging.

**Key findings**

- Age-related CD8 T cell dysfunction correlates with reduced chromatin accessibility of stemness genes
- Exhausted memory T: decreased accessibility of genes limiting postactivation differentiation
- Aged CD8 T cells show increased accessibility of terminal inhibitory genes in senescent and exhausted subsets

**Scientific significance**

This work identifies specific chromatin alterations driving CD8 T cell aging and reveals a novel regulatory T cell population that emerges as a hallmark of immune senescence.
