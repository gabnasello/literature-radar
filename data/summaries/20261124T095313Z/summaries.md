## A Device-to-Package Perspective on Thermal Management Materials for Electronic Devices.

PMID: 42968452 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42968452/

**Summary**

This review examines thermal management materials for electronic devices across device and package levels. It analyzes thermal transport mechanisms, material properties, and interface effects to identify bottlenecks in heat dissipation pathways. The study emphasizes that intrinsic thermal conductivity alone is insufficient; dimensions, processing, and interfaces critically influence thermal performance. Key bottlenecks and material requirements for high-power-density devices are defined.

**Key findings**

- Intrinsic thermal conductivity alone cannot determine thermal performance due to dimensional, processing, and interface effects
- Device-level materials (semiconductors, metals, dielectrics) and thermal interface materials (TIMs) are critical for heat dissipation
- Key bottlenecks in internal heat-dissipation pathways define material and interface requirements for high-power-density electronic devices

**Scientific significance**

This review establishes that thermal performance in high-power-density electronics depends on integrated material properties, interface engineering, and dimensional considerations beyond intrinsic thermal conductivity.

## Submolecular Selenium Enables Low-Pressure and Recyclable All-Solid-State Li-Se Batteries.

PMID: 42968454 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42968454/

**Summary**

This study investigates enhancing all-solid-state lithium-selenium batteries by engineering submolecular selenium species. The researchers converted crystalline cyclo-Se8 into amorphous Sex (x < 4) anchored on graphene, improving electronic conductivity and lithium diffusion. A Li2O-nanoshell-modified sulfide electrolyte stabilized interfaces. Batteries achieved 86% capacity retention after 2800 cycles at 3 C under 8 MPa and 420 Wh kg-1 energy density at 2.5 MPa, with recyclable selenium species post-cycling.

**Key findings**

- Submolecular Sex cathodes (x < 4) anchored on graphene increased electronic conductivity to 13 S cm-1
- Li2O-nanoshell-modified sulfide electrolyte stabilized interfaces with Li metal and Se cathode
- All-solid-state Li-Se batteries retained 86% capacity after 2800 cycles at 3 C under 8 MPa

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes submolecular chalcogen chemistry as a design strategy for low-pressure, recyclable, high-energy all-solid-state batteries.

- Partial thymectomy may preserve immunocompetence in oncologically feasible cases

**Scientific significance**

PMID: 42968462 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42968462/

**Summary**

This study investigates achieving reversible photo-charging in photo-rechargeable batteries using stacked 1T/2H MoS2 bifunctional cathodes. The researchers integrated photo-active 2H and energy-storage 1T MoS2 phases with nickel oxide to enhance conductivity and phase stability. An external resistance was used to balance self-discharge and photo-charging currents. The system demonstrated reversible photo-charging for over ten cycles with a 100% photo-charging capacity ratio within two hours of illumination, achieving 110.55 mAh g- 1 specific capacity.

**Key findings**

- Reversible photo-charging for over ten cycles using stacked 1T/2H MoS2 cathodes
- 100% photo-charging capacity ratio achieved within 2 hours of illumination
- 110.55 mAh g-1 specific capacity with zinc deposition at the anode

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes a method for genuine, reversible photo-charging in batteries without side reactions, advancing sustainable energy storage systems that directly convert solar energy into stored electrical energy.

## Remodeling of the tumor immune microenvironment and underlying oncogenic molecular mechanisms in HPV-associated OSCC.

PMID: 43048024 | Journal: American journal of cancer research
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43048024/

**Summary**

This study investigated HPV-positive oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) by correlating clinical parameters with immune and molecular markers. Retrospective analysis revealed significant differences in tumor site, differentiation, and 5-year survival between HPV-positive and negative OSCC. IHC showed downregulated p53 and Cyclin D1 in HPV-positive tumors, while MDSCs increased progressively from precancerous lesions to OSCC, and Tregs were elevated in OSCC patients. Transcriptomic analysis identified circRNA-miRNA axes targeting Calsequestrin 2 (CASQ2), suggesting immunosuppressive remodeling and a core pathogenic mechanism for HPV-positive OSCC progression.

**Key findings**

- HPV-positive OSCC patients exhibited significant differences in tumor site, pathological differentiation, and 5-year survival compared to HPV-negative OSCC
- HPV-positive OSCC tissues showed downregulated p53 and Cyclin D1 protein expression
- Two circRNA-miRNA regulatory axes specifically target Calsequestrin 2 (CASQ2) in HPV-positive OSCC

**Scientific significance**

This work identifies a novel circRNA-miRNA regulatory mechanism driving immunosuppressive remodeling in HPV-positive OSCC, providing a potential target for precision immunotherapy.

PMID: 42968464 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42968464/

**Summary**

This study investigates a bio-derived Janus membrane for selective aromatic recovery from saline industrial wastewaters. The membrane integrates a wood scaffold with a polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS) layer to enable soft-gated transport of aromatic compounds. Spectroscopic analyses confirm transient hydroxyl-mediated phenol capture and efficient diffusion through PD. Life-cycle assessments show over 85% environmental impact reduction and annual CO2 mitigation of 42,000 tons.

**Key findings**

- The membrane achieves 99.4% salt rejection and a phenol mass-transfer coefficient of 36.9 × 10-7 m s-1
- It demonstrates sustained performance in petrochemical and offshore oilfield wastewaters with a tensile strength of ∼70 MPa
- Life-cycle analyses indicate over 85% reductions in environmental impact and annual CO2 mitigation of 42,000 tons

**Scientific significance**

This bio-derived Janus membrane architecture enables efficient aromatic recovery from saline wastewaters while minimizing environmental impact, advancing sustainable resource recovery from industrial effluents.

## Chemically Reactivatable Sulfide Interfaces for Low-Pressure Thick-Cathode Solid-State Na-S Batteries.

PMID: 42968469 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42968469/

**Summary**

This study addresses the mechanical instability in low-pressure solid-state Na-S batteries with thick sulfur cathodes, where solid-solid contact loss impedes charge recovery. Researchers developed chemically reactivatable sulfide interfaces using Fe3C-MnO heteropoints in nitrogen-doped carbon nanotubes. The interface maintains electronic coupling and sulfide affinity, enabling sustained sulfur recovery under low pressure. At 0.2 MPa, a 200 µm cathode with 8.3 mg cm⁻² sulfur achieved 696 Wh kg⁻¹ energy density and stable cycling.

**Key findings**

- Fe3C-MnO heteropoints in nitrogen-doped carbon nanotubes create reactivatable sulfide interfaces
- The interface sustains sulfur recovery under low pressure (0.2 MPa) despite mechanical confinement
- A 200 µm cathode with 8.3 mg cm⁻² sulfur achieved 696 Wh kg⁻¹ energy density and stable cycling

**Scientific significance**

This work demonstrates a strategy to decouple reversible sulfur chemistry from mechanical constraints in solid-state Na-S batteries, enabling high-energy storage with thick cathodes under low operational pressure.

## Suprasilica Nanosieves for Selective Capture of Aromatics Over Cyclic Aliphatics in Hydrocarbon Feedstocks.

PMID: 42972981 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42972981/

**Summary**

This study investigates supramolecular silica nanosieves (suprasilica) for selectively separating aromatic hydrocarbons from cyclic aliphatics in hydrocarbon feedstocks. **Key findings**

- Suprasilica nanosieves achieve high selectivity for aromatics over cyclic aliphatics in hydrocarbon feedstocks
- Δ-SiNx sieves maintain selective separation performance in vapor, dynamic column, and liquid phases including crude oil
- Metadynamics simulations indicate kinetic selectivity mechanisms where aromatic hydrocarbons pass through macrocyclic apertures with lower barriers

**Scientific significance**

These suprasilica nanosieves provide a scalable, energy-efficient separation strategy for hydrocarbon purification with benchmark selectivity, advancing molecularly selective hydrocarbon separation technologies.
