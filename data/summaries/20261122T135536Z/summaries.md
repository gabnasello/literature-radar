## Self-Segregated Buried-Interface Regulation Resolves Kinetic-Environmental Coupling for Ambient Flexible Perovskite Solar Cells.

PMID: 42910623 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42910623/

**Summary**

This study investigates kinetic-environmental coupling at buried interfaces in ambient flexible perovskite solar cells, revealing simultaneous nucleation at dual interfaces during processing. By designing a self-segregating molecule (NFA), the researchers synchronize crystallization, passivate defects, and create hydrophobic barriers. The approach achieves record power conversion efficiencies (24.84% for small cells, 21.46% for modules) with stable performance after 1000 hours of damp aging.

**Key findings**

- Simultaneous nucleation occurs at both gas-liquid and liquid-solid interfaces during antisolvent processing
- Self-segregating NFA molecule synchronizes dual-interface crystallization and passivates defects
- Devices maintain 96.26% efficiency after 1000 hours of damp aging at 85±5% RH

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes self-segregated interfacial regulation as a general strategy to resolve kinetic-environmental trade-offs in ambient flexible perovskite solar cells, advancing stability and efficiency in scalable photovoltaic systems.

- Cage architectures maintain high iodine capture efficiency in competitive anion environments

**Scientific significance**
