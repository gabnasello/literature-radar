## Perinatal elimination of genetically aberrant human cortical neurons.

PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42951966/

**Summary**

This study investigated somatic copy number variants (CNVs) in human cortical neurons across development using single-cell whole-genome sequencing. Analysis of 2,357 neurons revealed 25.9% of prenatal cortical neurons had widespread CNVs, which decreased 4.5-fold postnatally. Perinatal elimination of neurons with high CN:CNV burdens occurred, likely linked to programmed cell death, with surviving neurons showing depletion of dosage-sensitive genes and neurodevelopmental disorder genes.

**Key findings**

- 25.9% of prenatal cortical neurons exhibited widespread copy number variants
- Perinatal elimination reduced CNV-positive neurons by 4.5-fold postnatally
- Surviving neurons showed depletion of dosage-sensitive genes and neurodevelopmental disorder genes

**Scientific significance**

The study demonstrates a perinatal genome quality-control mechanism eliminating neurons with aberrant genomes, providing insights into how genetic instability is managed during human neurodevelopment.

## Delayed electron-hole recombination in a pseudo-direct semiconductor.

PMID: 42951972 | Journal: Nature
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42951972/

**Summary**

This study investigates how semiconductor materials can circumvent Kirchhoff's reciprocity principle limiting solar energy conversion. Using bismuth sulfide, the researchers identified a pseudo-direct bandgap structure with a lower-energy indirect transition and a direct transition elevated by a few kBT. This configuration delays bimolecular electron-hole reusing, enhancing absorption and enabling potential improvements in solar energy conversion beyond Planck's law constraints.

**Key findings**

- Bismuth sulfide exhibits a pseudo-direct bandgap with a direct transition positioned several kBT above a lower-energy indirect transition
- This pseudo-direct bandgap structure significantly delays bimolecular electron-hole recombination compared to classic direct semiconductors
- The combination of high absorption and delayed recombination enables solar energy conversion beyond Planck's law limitations

**Scientific significance**

The discovery of a pseudo-direct bandgap in bismuth sulfide demonstrates a mechanism to circumvent Kirchhoff's reciprocity principle, potentially enabling more efficient solar energy conversion by overcoming fundamental limits imposed by Planck's law.
