## Piezoelectric electroporation potentiates high-frequency irreversible electroporation for enhanced prostate cancer eradication.

PMID: 42962342 | Journal: Biomaterials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42962342/

**Summary**

This study investigates whether piezoelectric electroporation can enhance high-frequency irreversible electroporation (H-FIRE) for prostate cancer treatment. Residual tumor cells after H-FIRE were targeted using a piezoelectric nanomaterial (BOIS) that delivers electric pulses and reactive oxygen species (ROS) to induce irreversible damage. The approach exploits DNA damage repair mechanisms to convert reversible injury into lethal outcomes, significantly improving tumor eradication without additional external energy sources.

**Key findings**

- BOIS nanomaterial delivers electric pulses and ROS to residual prostate cancer cells post-H-FIRE
- The dual mechanism of electroporation and oxidative stress converts reversible injury to irreversible cell death
- This strategy enhances H-FIRE efficacy by eliminating residual tumor cells without external energy input

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes a self-reinforcing therapeutic loop where focal ablation serves as a delivery mechanism for nanomaterials, enabling targeted elimination of residual tumor cells through intrinsic electric field and oxidative stress mechanisms.

## Topological control of polaritonic flatbands in anisotropic van der Waals metasurfaces.

PMID: 42957886 | Journal: Nature Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42957886/

**Summary**

This study investigates how anisotropic van der Waals materials, specifically rhenium disulfide (ReS2), can be engineered into metasurfaces to control photonic band topology. The researchers reshaped ReS2's band structure to split topological charges into half-integer polarization singularities, creating directional flatbands. These flatbands hybridize with ReS2's excitonic transitions to form polarized exciton-polaritons, enabling polarization-resolved quantum emitters and low-threshold polariton devices.

**Key findings**

- Structuring ReS2 into a resonant metasurface splits integer topological charges into half-integer polarization singularities
- The engineered flatbands hybridize with ReS2's linearly polarized excitonic transitions to form directionally polarized exciton-polaritons
- This approach enables scalable polarization-resolved quantum emitters and flatband-enhanced nonlinear optics

**Study type**

Metasurface

**Scientific significance**

This work demonstrates a pathway to manipulate photonic band topology in 2D materials for directional light-matter interactions, advancing the design of polariton-based nanophotonic devices with tailored polarization properties.

## An autonomous macrophage-nociceptor mechanosensory relay drives chronic visceral pain.

PMID: 42913023 | Journal: Science Immunology
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42913023/

**Summary**

This study investigates whether immune cells directly transduce mechanical cues into nociceptive signals during ulcerative colitis. Using a minimally inflammatory zymosan model, researchers identified Piezo1 in CX3CR1+ macrophages as a mechanosensor that triggers NGF synthesis via calcium influx and NF-κB nuclear translocation. This macrophage-derived NGF sensitizes TRPV1+ nociceptors, establishing a direct mechanosensory relay independent of cytokine cascades, which reduces visceral hyperalgesia when genetically disrupted.

**Key findings**

- Piezo1 in CX3CR1+ macrophages directly transduces mechanical cues into nociceptive signals during colitis
- Piezo1 activation induces NGF synthesis through calcium influx and NF-κB nuclear translocation
- Genetic disruption of Piezo1, Ngf, or Ntrk1 abolishes visceral hyperalgesia in colitis models

**Study type**

minimally inflammatory intracolonic zymosan model

**Scientific significance**

This work identifies a direct mechanosensory pathway from tissue-resident macrophages to nociceptors that operates independently of inflammatory cytokines, providing a novel mechanism for visceral pain in colitis.

## Mitochondrial DNA abundance is a determinant of immunogenic cell death in cancer.

PMID: 42962666 | Journal: Immunity
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42962666/

**Summary**

This study investigates how mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) abundance influences immunogenic cell death (ICD) in cancer. Using KRAS-mutant cancer models, it demonstrates that MEK inhibitors induce mtDNA release but are counteracted by PRKN-mediated mitophagy. Inhibiting mitophagy enhances mtDNA efflux, amplifying cGAS-STING signaling and promoting CD8+ T cell responses. The balance between mt:cytochrome c release dictates ICD versus apoptosis outcomes, with clinical relevance in human tumors.

**Key findings**

- MEK inhibitors induce mtDNA release in KRAS-mutant cancer cells but are limited by PRKN-mediated mitophagy
- Inhibiting mitophagy increases mtDNA efflux, enhancing cGAS-STING signaling and CD8+ T cell activation
- PRKN expression negatively correlates with immune infiltration in human cancers

**Study type**

Preclinical models with pharmacological screening

**Scientific significance**

The findings establish a mechanistic link between mitochondrial dynamics, mtDNA release, and immunogenic cell death, providing a targetable pathway to enhance antitumor immunity through dual inhibition of mitochondrial stress pathways.

## Functional architecture of the human insula.

PMID: 42907497 | Journal: Nature
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42907497/

**Summary**

This study identifies a quadripartite functional architecture of the human insula using intracranial electrical stimulation and electroencephalography in 87 participants. It defines four regions: dorsal-posterior for nociceptive/thermoceptive signals, ventral-posterior for somatic information, mid-anterior for visceral sensations and anxiety, and anterior-polar for salience detection. The anterior region shows strong connections with the prefrontal cortex, enabling cognitive modulation of pain and bodily perception.

**Key findings**

- The insula has four distinct functional regions: dorsal-posterior encoding nociceptive/thermoceptive signals, ventral-posterior processing somatic information across body parts, mid-anterior linked to visceral sensations and anxiety, and anterior-polar involved in salience detection.
- The anterior-polar region exhibits strong connections with the prefrontal cortex, including the anterior cingulate cortex, exerting direct influence over other insular regions.
- This architecture links cytoarchitecture to asymmetric electrophysiological interactions, providing mechanistic insights into cognitive modulation of pain and interoception.

**Study type**

Intracranial electrical stimulation and electroencephalography

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes a functional framework for the human insula that connects cytoarchitectonic organization with directed electrophysiological pathways, advancing understanding of how cognition modulates pain and bodily perception.
