## PKA signaling controls ILC3 homeostasis and IL-23-mediated inflammation in the gut.

PMID: 43015493 | Journal: Nature Immunology
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43015493/

**Summary**

This study investigates how protein kinase A (PKA) signaling regulates group 3 innate lymphoid cell (ILC3) homeostasis and inflammation in the gut. Using a single-cell atlas, researchers found that prostaglandin activates PKA to promote tissue-protective functions, while interleukin-23 (IL-23) suppresses PKA inhibitors. Deletion of PRKAR1A reduced ILC3s and increased infection susceptibility in mice, and human ILC3s express PRKAR1A, which is downregulated by IL-23 in inflammation.

**Key findings**

- IL-23 decreases PKA inhibitors in ILC3s, promoting inflammation
- PRKAR1A deletion in ILC3s reduces cell numbers and increases enteric infection risk
- Human intestinal ILC3s express PRKAR1A, downregulated by IL-23 in inflammatory bowel disease

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes PKA signaling as a critical regulator of ILC3-mediated gut homeostasis and identifies IL-23-driven PKA dysregulation as a mechanism for intestinal inflammation.

## Maternal and early life obesity programs CD4+ T cell fate to impair vaccine immunity.

PMID: 43040049 | Journal: Nature Immunology
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43040049/

**Summary**

This study investigated how maternal and early life obesity impacts adaptive immune responses in children. Using a mouse model replicating the obesity-epidemiological link, researchers found that high-fat diet exposure during pregnancy or early postnatal life, but not adulthood, altered naive CD4+ T cell epigenetics and transcription, impairing T follicular helper cell differentiation and vaccine responses. The mechanism involved epigenetic repression of A20 (Tnfaip3).

**Key findings**

- High-fat diet during pregnancy or early postnatal life reshapes naive CD4+ T cell epigenetics and transcription
- Impaired T follicular helper cell differentiation and germinal center reactions in response to vaccines
- Epigenetic repression of A20 (Tnfaip3) drives these immune defects

**Scientific significance**

This work identifies maternal and early life obesity as critical determinants of adaptive immune function during a developmental window, providing mechanistic insights into how obesity programs immune responses that affect vaccine efficacy.

**Scientific significance**

- Forced ESCRT-0 recruitment and SMO clustering reconstitute EV budding

**Scientific significance**

**Scientific significance**
