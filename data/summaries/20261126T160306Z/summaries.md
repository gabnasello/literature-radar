## Staged Network Evolution Enables In Situ Strengthening and On-Demand Recyclability of Polyurethanes via Dynamic Sulfilimine-Urea Bonds.

PMID: 43010581 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43010581/

**Summary**

This study investigates a staged network evolution strategy using dynamic sulfilimine-urea bonds to enhance polyurethane mechanical properties while enabling recyclability. The approach involves forming initial solvent-resistant polyurethane (SUP-S1) via rapid isocyanate addition to sulfilimines, followed by thermal treatment to regenerate sulfilimines and form stable urethane bonds (SUP-S2), yielding improved tensile strength (17→42 MPa) and toughness (55→158 MJ m-3). Subsequent photocleavage of sulfilimines allows on-demand recyclability, retaining >96% mechanical properties after five cycles. The design achieves enhanced performance and recyclability without compromising stability.

**Key findings**

- Initial polyurethane (SUP-S1) formed via rapid isocyanate addition to sulfilimines
- Thermal treatment converts SUP-S1 to SUP-S2 with enhanced tensile strength (17→42 MPa) and toughness (55→158 MJ m-3)
- Photocleavage of sulfilimines enables multi-recyclability with >96% mechanical retention after five cycles

**Scientific significance**

This work demonstrates a unified design principle for cross-linked polymers that simultaneously improves mechanical performance and end-of-life recyclability through controlled network evolution, advancing sustainable polymer engineering.

## A Janus Bilayer Portland Cement-Based Moisture-Electricity Generator via Hydrogen-Bonding Network Regulation.

PMID: 43010605 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43010605/

**Summary**

This study investigates enhancing moisture-electricity generation (MEG) in cement-based materials by regulating hydrogen-bonding networks. Using asymmetric acid etching, researchers created a Janus bilayer Portland cement generator (CMEG) that enables unidirectional water diffusion. The CMEG achieved high short-circuit current (723 µA/cm²) and power density (112.5 µW/cm²) while maintaining mechanical strength and scalability, addressing limitations in current MEG materials through optimized hydrogen-bonding dynamics.

**Key findings**

- CMEG achieves 723 µA/cm² short-circuit current density
- Output power density reaches 112.5 µW/cm²
- Janus bilayer structure enables unidirectional water diffusion

**Scientific significance**

This work demonstrates a cement-based platform for high-performance moisture-electricity generation through hydrogen-bonding network engineering, advancing the field of self-powered building energy harvesting.

## Deciphering Roles of Interfacial Tunable Primer for High Active Material Contents Pure Silicon Electrodes.

PMID: 43010621 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43010621/

**Summary**

This study investigates enhancing pure silicon anodes for lithium-ion batteries by mitigating volumetric expansion-induced degradation. Researchers designed an interfacial tunable primer (fp) that improves mechanical resilience and electrochemical cohesion between electrode components. The fp layer dissipates interfac. stress and maintains electron conduction pathways while regulating ion flux via benzoxazine frameworks. The modified Si anodes achieved 99 wt% active material content with stable cycling, avoiding typical degradation in binder-free compositions and demonstrating viability in pouch cells paired with NCM90 cathodes.

**Key findings**

- The interfacial tunable primer (fp) enabled 99 wt% active material content in pure silicon anodes without significant degradation
- The fp layer dissipated interfacial stress and maintained continuous electron conduction pathways
- Benzoxazine frameworks in the fp regulated interfacial solvation structure and enhanced ion flux

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes a molecular-level engineering strategy for stabilizing pure silicon anodes at high active material fractions, advancing the practical implementation of silicon-based high-energy-density lithium-ion batteries.

## Intranasal Pathogenesis-Adaptive Nanotherapeutics Reinstate Regenerative Competence for Post-Stroke Neural Repair.

PMID: 43010623 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43010623/

**Summary**

This study investigates enhancing post-stroke neural repair by addressing neuronal regenerative limitations. Using transcriptomic analysis, it identified redox imbalance and impaired nitric oxide signaling as key barriers. The researchers developed FCeO2@GLp, a nanotherapeutic that delivers reactive oxygen species scavenging, nitric oxide replenishment, and immune modulation via intranasal administration. The treatment reduced infarct volume, cerebral edema, and improved neurological outcomes by promoting neural stem cell proliferation and neuronal differentiation in the acute phase.

**Key findings**

- FCeO2@GLp reduces infarct volume and cerebral edema through intranasal delivery
- The nanotherapeutic releases cerium oxide nanozyme, fingolimod, and a nitric oxide donor in response to local acidity
- It enhances neural stem cell proliferation and neuronal differentiation, improving long-term neurological function

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes a pathogenesis-adaptive nanotherapeutic strategy that dynamically targets multiple regenerative barriers in stroke recovery, offering a novel approach to enhance neural repair without systemic side effects.

## Bridge-Level Electronic Compensation in Prussian White Cathodes: Charge-Transfer Kinetic Regimes and Design Principles.

PMID: 43010630 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43010630/

**Summary**

This study investigates electronic compensation mechanisms in Prussian White (PW) cathodes for sodium-ion batteries. The research proposes a bridge-level framework focusing on the Fe─C≡N─TM bridge unit to describe local charge redistribution. Using Marcus-type descriptors, it identifies three key contributions: local charge-state free-energy difference (ΔG°), cyanide-mediated electronic coupling (Hab), and structure-coupled re. The framework categorizes kinetic regimes based on energetic mismatch, electronic coupling, and structural reorganization, offering design principles for optimizing PW cathodes through redox alignment, coupling, and structural control.

**Key findings**

- The Fe─C≡N─TM bridge unit serves as a local descriptor for charge redistribution in PW cathodes
- Three Marcus-type descriptors define electronic compensation: ΔG°, Hab, and λ
- Kinetic regimes are organized by balancing energetic mismatch, electronic coupling, and structural reorganization

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes a physical framework for understanding and designing Prussian White cathodes by linking local electronic compensation mechanisms to structural and kinetic behavior, advancing sodium-ion battery material optimization.

## Synergistic Dual-Site Regulation in a Tailored Imine-COF for Enhanced Hydrogen Peroxide Photosynthesis.

PMID: 43010674 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43010674/

**Summary**

This study investigates hierarchical nitrogen coordination engineering in covalent organic frameworks (COFs) to enhance hydrogen peroxide production via oxygen reduction reactions. By tailoring triazine and pyridine sites, researchers developed TAPT-BTPA, which improved charge separation and visible-light absorption. Subsequent anchoring of antimony at pyridine-imine sites created TAPT-BTPA-Sb, redirecting the oxygen reduction pathway toward a stepwise 1e- mechanism. This synergistic dual-site regulation increased hydrogen peroxide production rates from 8.12 to 15.3 mmol g-1 h-1 in aqueous solution with isopropanol.

**Key findings**

- Tailoring nitrogen coordination environments (triazine vs. pyridine) in COFs improved charge separation and visible-light absorption for hydrogen peroxide production
- Antimony anchoring at pyridine-imine sites redirected the oxygen reduction pathway to a stepwise 1e- mechanism
- Synergistic dual-site regulation increased hydrogen peroxide production rates from 8.12 to 15.3 mmol g-1 h-1 under identical conditions

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes a molecular-level design strategy for optimizing covalent organic frameworks through dual-site coordination engineering to achieve enhanced hydrogen peroxide synthesis via controlled oxygen reduction pathways.

## Bioinspired Buffered Microenvironment Enables Highly Crystalline 3D COF Gels With Controllable Structures for Exceptional Molecular Adsorption.

PMID: 43012106 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43012106/

**Summary**

This study addresses the challenge of achieving long-range crystallinity in 3D covalent organic framework (COF) gels by developing a bioinspired buffered system. Using ten metal salts to regulate pH microenvironments, researchers identified sodium acetate as an optimal regulator that maintains weakly acidic conditions, promotes structural order, and stabilizes the gel network. The approach enables crystalline phase switching via side substituents and salt addition, yielding gels with high CO2/N2 selectivity, exceptional mechanical properties, and robust adsorption capacity.

**Key findings**

- Sodium acetate maintains a stable weakly acidic microenvironment to promote crystalline order in COF gels
- The system enables switching between two crystalline phases via side substituents and salt addition sequence
- Gels exhibit high CO2/N2 selectivity (140), low density (0.028-0.042 g/cm³), and >90% capacity retention after 20 cycles

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes a strategy for engineering crystalline 3D COF gels through microenvironment control and ion selection, advancing materials design for high-performance adsorption applications.

## Lewis Acid-Activated High-Entropy Layered Double Hydroxide for Superior Seawater Electrolysis.

PMID: 43012116 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43012116/

**Summary**

This study investigates enhancing seawater electrolysis efficiency by integrating Zn2+ as a Lewis acid into high-entropy layered double hydroxides (LDHs). The approach modulates interfacial microenvironments and bulk electronic structures to address sluggish anodic kinetics and chloride corrosion. Results show catalysts requiring 35 and 160 mV overpotentials for hydrogen and oxygen evolution at 10 mA cm-2, with small Tafel slopes and 900-hour stability in anion-exchange membrane electrolyzers. The mechanism involves Zn2+ preferentially adsorbing OH- to repel Cl- and creating oxygen vacancies that optimize metal sites.

**Key findings**

- Zn2+ incorporation into LDHs reduces chloride-induced corrosion by preferentially adsorbing OH-
- The catalyst achieves 35 mV overpotential for hydrogen evolution at 10 mA cm-2
- 900-hour operational stability in anion-exchange membrane electrolyzers at 1.5 A cm-2

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes a dual-regulation strategy using Lewis acid doping to design robust catalysts for seawater electrolysis, advancing sustainable green hydrogen production in corrosive environments.

## Plasmonically Enhanced Microarray Immunoassay for High-Sensitivity Monitoring of Heart Failure.

PMID: 43015983 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43015983/

**Summary**

This study addresses the tradeoff between analytical sensitivity and cost in point-of-care testing by developing a plasmonically enhanced fluorescence microarray immunoassay. Using metal-dielectric-metal nanopatch antennas, it achieves up to 485-fold fluorescence enhancement for detecting heart failure biomarkers (NT-proBNP, NGAL, galectin-3) with low-cost smartphone-based detection. The platform demonstrates strong correlation with clinical assays and low detection limits (0.055–0.864 ng/mL), enabling high-sensitivity, multiplexed heart failure monitoring in resource-limited settings.

**Key findings**

- Plasmonic nanopatch antennas provide up to 485-fold fluorescence enhancement
- Quantitative detection of three heart failure biomarkers with limits of detection of 0.055, 3.932, and 0.864 ng/mL
- Strong correlation between results and clinically validated assays for NT-proBNP-positive samples

**Scientific significance**

This approach enables high-sensitivity, multiplexed biomarker detection at low cost, addressing a critical tradeoff in point-of-care diagnostics while maintaining clinical relevance for heart failure monitoring.
