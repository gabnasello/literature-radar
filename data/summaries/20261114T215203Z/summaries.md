## Industrialization drives convergent microbiome and host physiological shifts in the human metaorganism.

PMID: 42971202 | Journal: Cell
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42971202/

**Summary**

This study investigates industrialization's impact on the human metaorganism by analyzing gut microbiome, immunoglobulin A, host genotyping, diet, lifestyle, and fecal biomarkers across globally diverse populations. It demonstrates industrialization correlates with homogenized microbial compositions, reduced diversity, lower community stability, elevated gut stress markers, increased IgA secretion, and altered IgA-bacteria interactions. Microbiome disease predictors from industrialized groups fail in less industrialized cohorts, indicating limited cross-population applicability.

**Key findings**

- Industrialization correlates with homogenized microbial compositions
- Industrialization is linked to reduced microbial diversity and lower community stability
- Microbiome disease predictors trained on industrialized populations are uninformative in less industrialized cohorts

**Scientific significance**

The findings reveal coordinated restructuring of the microbial ecosystem and host physiology under industrialization, emphasizing the need for globally representative data to improve translational microbiome applications across diverse human populations.

## CRISPR mutagenesis of the MYC locus reveals a druggable ultraconserved RNA element.

PMID: 42982038 | Journal: Cell
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42982038/

**Summary**

This study used CRISPR mutagenesis to map functional elements within the MYC gene locus at base-pair resolution. It found 67% of essential base pairs are noncoding, with an ultraconserved RNA element in the 3' UTR critical for MYC protein localization. Targeting this element with ASOs selectively kills cancer cells and inhibits tumor growth, highlighting noncoding sequences as key functional regulators and identifying a druggable target.

**Key findings**

- 67% of functionally essential base pairs in the MYC locus are noncoding
- An ultraconserved noncoding RNA element in the MYC 3' UTR promotes perinuclear mRNA localization and nuclear protein localization
- Steric-blocking ASOs targeting this RNA element selectively eliminate MYC-dependent cancer cells and inhibit tumor organoid growth

**Study type**

CRISPR tiling mutagenesis

**Scientific significance**

The study demonstrates that noncoding sequences are major carriers of functional genetic information and identifies a druggable RNA element that disrupts MYC-driven cancer mechanisms.

PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42904536/

- The additive enhances Zn2+ transport and desolvation kinetics, reducing migration energy barriers

**Study type**

Electrolyte additive
