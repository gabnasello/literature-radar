## Ultralow Loading Iridium Doped Hollandite Manganese Oxide Nanowires for Efficient Acidic Oxygen Evolution Reaction.

PMID: 43004155 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43004155/

{
  "summary": "This study investigates ultralow-loading iridium-doped hollandite manganese oxide nanowires as efficient acidic oxygen evolution reaction (OER) catalysts. The researchers integrated 3 at% iridium into a manganese dioxide framework, reducing the overpotential at 10 mA cm-2 from 580 mV to 303 mV compared to undoped α-MnO2. The catalyst demonstrates significantly higher iridium mass activity than commercial IrOx. Dissolution measurements and spectroscopic analyses revealed modest material loss (79% Ir, 76% Mn retained after 6 h), with iridium remaining oxidized and bound to the framework while manganese undergoes pH- and potential-dependent reduction and structural changes. The findings emphasize the importance of evaluating dissolution, reconstruction, and oxidation state together for designing durable iridium-efficient catalysts.",
  "key_findings": [
    "Ultralow-loading iridium (3 at%, <16 µgIr cm-2) in hollandite MnO2 reduces OER overpotential at 10 mA cm-2 from 580 mV to 303 mV",
    "The catalyst achieves iridium mass activity an order of magnitude higher than commercial IrOx",
    "Modest dissolution (79% Ir, 76% Mn retained after 6 h) with iridium remaining oxidized and associated with the framework"
  ],
  "scientific_significance": "This work demonstrates that ultralow-loading iridium-doped hollandite manganese oxides can achieve high OER activity with minimal iridium, providing a pathway to more sustainable and efficient catalysts for acidic environments."

## Diet at disease onset determines tissue inflammatory memory.

PMID: 42957863 | Journal: Nature
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42957863/

{
  "summary": "This study investigates whether brief dietary changes during acute inflammation influence long-term tissue immune memory. Researchers exposed lean mice to a high-fat diet (HFD) at the onset of type 17 skin inflammation and found that a 4-day window of HFD exposure exacerbated disease severity comparably to chronic obesity. HFD outside this window had no effect, while switching obese mice to a low-fat diet during the window reduced inflammation. Mechanistically, HFD recruited neutrophils, which expressed Nlrp3 and Il1b, driving pathogenic TH17 cell activation. Post-recovery, a chromatin-poised T cell memory persisted, causing exaggerated inflammation upon recurrence despite subsequent LFD. The findings suggest transient dietary states during inflammation can durably reprogram immune responses.",
  "key_findings": [
    "Transient HFD exposure during a 4-day window at disease onset exacerbates skin inflammation comparably to chronic obesity",
    "HFD outside this window has no impact, but switching obese mice to LFD during the window blunts disease amplification",
    "Neutrophil-mediated Nlrp3-Il1b signaling licenses pathogenic TH17 cells, with persistent chromatin-poised T cell memory after recovery"
  ],
  "scientific_significance": "The study demonstrates that short-lived dietary states during acute inflammation can durably reprogram tissue immune memory, revealing a critical temporal window for dietary interventions to influence long-term inflammatory responses."
}
