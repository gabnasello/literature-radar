## Bridging the Gap Between Photocatalytic Water Oxidation Half-Reaction and Stoichiometric Pure Water Splitting Over Ta3N5 via Cocatalyst Engineering.

PMID: 42920390 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42920390/

**Summary**

This study addresses the challenge of achieving stable stoichiometric pure water splitting over Ta3N5 by identifying incompatibility between cocatalysts in alkaline water oxidation and pure water splitting as a critical bottleneck. Using a multi-step incipient wetness impregnation strategy, researchers developed a highly dispersed RuCoOx cocatalyst. The optimized Pt@Cr2O3/Ta3N5/RuCoOx system achieved 0.72% apparent quantum yield at 420 nm for pure water splitting, overcoming photocorrosion-induced N2 generation through enhanced hole extraction and reduced reaction barriers at the interface.

**Key findings**

- Identification of cocatalyst incompatibility between alkaline water oxidation and pure water splitting as a bottleneck causing photocorrosion and N2 generation
- Development of a highly dispersed RuCoOx cocatalyst via multi-step incipient wetness impregnation
- Achievement of 0.72% apparent quantum yield at 420 nm for stoichiometric pure water splitting with Pt@Cr2O3/Ta3N5/RuCoOx

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes cocatalyst compatibility as a fundamental design principle for stable photocatalytic pure water splitting, providing mechanistic insights for advancing nitride and oxynitride photocatalysts.

## Guilty by marker? Reassessing the pro-tumor reputation of antigen-presenting M2-like macrophages.

PMID: 43035126 | Journal: Critical reviews in oncology/hematology
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43035126/

**Summary**

This review reassesses the pro-tumor role of M2-like tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) by evaluating functional evidence from in vitro and tumor-derived studies. It examines phagocytosis, antigen processing, cross-presentation, and CD8+ T cell activation. Findings indicate that some CD206+ TAMs can cross-present tumor antigens and activate CD8+ T cells comparably to dendritic cells, correlating with improved outcomes. Conversely, other macrophages may promote immunosuppression. The study emphasizes that antigen fate and co-stimulatory context, not M1/M2 markers, determine functional outcomes.

**Key findings**

- CD206+ tumor-associated macrophages can cross-present tumor antigens and activate CD8+ T cells at levels comparable to conventional dendritic cells
- These macrophages correlate with better clinical outcomes in specific contexts
- Antigen-presenting macrophages may either activate cytotoxic T cells or promote regulatory T cell activity depending on co-stimulatory context

**Scientific significance**

This review establishes that functional capacity, not M1/M2 marker expression, determines the immunological role of tumor-associated macrophages in cancer, shifting the focus from static markers to dynamic antigen-presenting mechanisms.

## Stone and water governed early human settlement of the desert.

PMID: 42951957 | Journal: Nature
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42951957/

**Summary**

This study investigates the factors influencing early human settlement in desert regions, focusing on the role of lithic raw materials beyond water availability. Using a 1,500-km geoarchaeological survey across the Rub' al Khali desert, researchers found no stone tools or knappable materials despite abundant freshwater at 55 of 125 sites. Spatial modeling indicates settlement required co-occurrence of water and lithic resources, with 57% of water-accessible terrain lacking suitable stone. The findings suggest that raw-material geology critically constrains early human dispersal, challenging models that prioritize water alone.

**Key findings**

- No lithic artefacts or knappable raw materials were found along a 1,500-k:km survey transect despite abundant freshwater
- 57% of water-accessible terrain lacks knappable stone, indicating lithic resource constraints
- Spatial modeling shows settlement tracked co-occurrence of water and lithic resources, not water alone

**Scientific significance**

The study demonstrates that lithic raw material availability, not water, was a critical constraint on early human dispersal across arid regions, revealing a previously overlooked geological factor in human migration models.

## Melting and resistivity of iron at Earth core conditions using pulsed Joule heating.

PMID: 43000118 | Journal: Science
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43000118/

**Summary**

This study investigates iron's melting temperature and electrical resistivity under Earth core pressures using microsecond pulsed Joule heating in diamond anvil cells. Over 300 reproducible melting measurements reveal a shallow melting curve, reaching 6 K/GPa and  4160 ± 80 K at 200 GPa. The findings suggest a relatively cold core-mantle boundary (3430 K) and low adiabatic outer core heat flow (9.8 TW), indicating thermal convection alone could power the geodynamo before inner core formation and that the lowermost mantle remains fully solid.

**Key findings**

- A shallow melting curve for iron at 200 GPa, reaching 4160 ± 80 K
- Inferred core-mantle boundary temperature of 3430 K
- Low adiabatic outer core heat flow of 9.8 TW

**Scientific significance**

The study provides critical constraints on iron's melting behavior and resistivity at high pressures, advancing understanding of Earth's core thermal evolution and geodynamo mechanisms.

## 4f-Electron-Regulated Dual-Periodic Bonding Evolution Across the Lanthanide Series.

PMID: 42947628 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42947628/

**Summary**

This study investigates how 4f electron filling influences bonding evolution in lanthanide single-atom catalysts across the lanthanide series. Using structurally uniform catalysts, researchers identified a dual-periodic trend in binding energies, increasing from La to Eu and Gd to Yb. The trend arises from electron redistribution between 5d and 4f orbitals, with a sharp transition at the half-filled 4f7 configuration (Eu to Gd). The findings demonstrate intrinsic electronic behavior across coordination environments, providing a framework for understanding lanthanide reactivity and catalyst design.

**Key findings**

- A dual-periodic trend in lanthanide-support binding energies increases from La to Eu and Gd to Yb
- Half-filled 4f7 configuration causes an abrupt transition between Eu and Gd
- Electron redistribution between 5d and 4f orbitals modulates bonding energies across diverse coordination environments

**Scientific significance**

The study establishes a fundamental 4f-electron-regulated bonding framework that explains intrinsic reactivity patterns in lanthanides, advancing understanding of how electronic structure governs catalytic behavior across the series.

## Regulatory T-Cell Dynamics During and After Direct Acting Antivirals in Cirrhotic HCV Genotype 4 Patients.

PMID: 43036170 | Journal: Journal of viral hepatitis
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43036170/

**Summary**

This study investigated regulatory T-cell dynamics in cirrhotic HCV genotype 4 patients during and after direct-acting antiviral (DAA) therapy. Using longitudinal flow cytometry, researchers compared 50 cirrhotic patients and 50 healthy controls at baseline, week 4, end of treatment, and 1 year post-SVR. Findings revealed baseline immune dysregulation with reduced T cells, profound CD8+ exhaustion, elevated activation markers, and expanded Tregs and TH17 cells. By 1 year post-SVR, exhausted CD8+ cells decreased by 66.2%, Tregs normalized, and TH1/TH2 subsets increased, indicating partial immune recovery after viral clearance.

**Key findings**

- Baseline cirrhotic patients showed reduced CD3+ T cells and profound CD8+ T-cell exhaustion (PD-1+ 71.0% vs. 11.1%)
- Tregs and TH17 cells were significantly elevated at baseline (52.7% vs. 15.1% and 25.8% vs. 14.0%)
- By 1 year post-SVR, exhausted PD-1+ CD8+ cells declined by 66.2%, Tregs normalized, and TH1/TH2 subsets increased

**Scientific significance**

The study demonstrates that successful DAA therapy in cirrhotic HCV genotype 4 patients induces partial correction of peripheral T-cell dysregulation, providing insights into immune recovery mechanisms post-viral clearance in chronic liver disease.

## Atmospheric O2 reveals a strengthening ocean carbon sink and a stagnant net land sink since 1990.

PMID: 43019429 | Journal: Science
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43019429/

**Summary**

This study quantifies the global carbon sinks using atmospheric oxygen and CO2 data from 1990 to 2019. It reveals a strengthening ocean carbon sink at 2.8 ± 0.4 Pg C year-1, accounting for 35% of fossil emissions, while the net land sink shows no significant trend (1.3 ± 0.5 Pg C year-1). The findings challenge existing model simulations, indicating potential inaccuracies in predicting future climate responses.

**Key findings**

- The ocean carbon sink strengthened by 2.8 ± 0.4 Pg C year-1 between 1990 and 2019
- The net land sink exhibited no robust trend despite large decadal variability
- The results contradict process-based model simulations of land and ocean sinks

**Scientific significance**

The study provides critical empirical constraints on carbon sink dynamics, enhancing understanding of how natural systems absorb anthropogenic CO2 emissions and challenging model projections for climate mitigation strategies.

## Life-Cycle-Sustainable Capacitive Deionization Enabled by Organic Molecular Engineering for Selective Ammonium Capture.

PMID: 42953939 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42953939/

**Summary**

This study investigates enhancing capacitive deionization (CDI) for selective ammonium recovery from wastewater using molecularly engineered organic materials. Researchers designed 1,4-bis(1H-imidazo[4,5-b]phenazin-2-yl)benzene (IPB) to overcome the capacity-selectivity trade-off in CDI. The IPB-based CDI achieved high ammonium adsorption capacity (267.23 mg g⁻¹) and selectivity in complex streams, reducing ammonium to below 3 mg L⁻¹ and enabling 82.3% recovery as fertilizer. It also demonstrated low carbon emissions (4.66 × 10⁻² t CO₂-eq per tonne NH₄⁺ treated).

**Key findings**

- IPB-based CDI achieved 267.23 mg g⁻¹ ammonium adsorption capacity
- The system maintained high selectivity in complex wastewater matrices
- CDI reduced ammonium to below 3 mg L⁻¹ with 82.3% fertilizer recovery

**Scientific significance**

This work demonstrates a molecularly engineered organic electrode material that enables high-capacity, selective ammonium capture from wastewater with low carbon footprint, advancing sustainable electrochemical water treatment technologies.

- No significant differences in transcriptomic profiles were observed between 5% and 10% DMSO conditions

**Scientific significance**

This work demonstrates that reduced-DMSO cryopreservation strategies can maintain critical functional and molecular characteristics of therapeutic nTreg products, advancing the feasibility of cryopreserved Treg therapies for clinical use.

## Nb5+-Induced-CeO2 Lattice Strain for Robust and Scalable Solar-Driven Ammonia Synthesis.

PMID: 42954773 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42954773/

**Summary**

This study investigates solar-driven ammonia synthesis using a 5 wt% Ru/Nb-CeO2 catalyst. By incorporating Nb5+ into CeO2, the catalyst induces compressive microstrain, suppressing oxygen sublattice reduction and maintaining Ru dispersion. In situ spectroscopy and computational analyses confirm an electron-enriched Ru state under reaction conditions. The catalyst achieves 7.4 mmol g-1 h-1 NH3 synthesis at 350°C with 144 h stability, demonstrating enhanced N2 activation and hydrogenation kinetics. The findings highlight lattice strain engineering as a viable strategy for robust, scalable solar ammonia production.

**Key findings**

- Nb5+ incorporation into CeO2 induces compressive microstrain, suppressing oxygen sublattice reduction
- In situ results confirm an electron-enriched Ru state under reaction conditions
- 5 wt% Ru/Nb-CeO2 achieves 7.4 mmol g-1 h-1 NH3 synthesis with 144 h stability

**Scientific significance**

The study establishes lattice strain engineering as a strategy to simultaneously stabilize oxygen sublattice and electronically promote Ru for efficient solar-driven ammonia synthesis.

## Evolutionary dynamics in the years preceding acute myeloid leukaemia.

PMID: 43015468 | Journal: Nature
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43015468/

**Summary**

This study quantifies pre-cancerous evolutionary dynamics in acute myeloid leukaemia using serial blood samples collected up to 15 years before diagnosis. By deep duplex sequencing and evolutionary modeling, it identifies early driver mutations occurring in the first few decades of life within highly polyclonal blood. These slow-growing clones later acquire multiple driver mutations with up to 100-fold greater selective advantages, leading to somatic sweeps decades prior to diagnosis. The findings reveal simple evolutionary rules balancing stochastic and deterministic processes in pre-leukaemic evolution.

**Key findings**

- Early driver mutations arise in the first few decades of life within highly polyclonal blood
- Subsequent driver mutations confer up to 100-fold greater selective advantages than early drivers
- Clonal sweeps of blood occur decades before diagnosis, strongly linked to future acute myeloid leukaemia

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes simple evolutionary rules governing pre-cancerous dynamics in acute myeloid leukaemia, revealing how stochastic and deterministic processes balance to drive clonal expansion prior to malignancy.
