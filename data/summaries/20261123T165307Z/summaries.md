## Fundamental Inhibition of Polyiodide Formation by a Site-Separated Binder for Highly Stable Aqueous Zinc-Iodine Batteries.

PMID: 42957568 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42957568/

**Summary**

This study investigates strategies to stabilize aqueous zinc-iodine batteries by inhibiting polyiodide formation, a key limitation. Using a site-separated polymer (SSP) binder, the researchers selectively confined iodine and iodide ions at distinct functional groups, disrupting polyiodide generation pathways. The SSP cathode achieved high capacity retention (97.1% over 31,000 cycles) and superior performance at high currents and iodine loadings compared to conventional binders.

**Key findings**

- SSP binder selectively confines I2 and I- at different functional groups to disrupt polyiodide formation
- I2/SSP cathode retains 97.1% capacity over 31,000 cycles at 20 A g-1
- At 50 mg cm-2 iodine loading, SSP cathode maintains 10.1 mAh cm-2 with 97.9% retention over 400 cycles

**Scientific significance**

The site-separated polymer binder provides a fundamental mechanism to suppress polyiodide shuttling in aqueous zinc-iodine batteries, enhancing long-term stability and high-rate performance without compromising capacity.

Responders exhibited distinct tertiary lymphoid structures with reduced DC-Treg proximity, while non-responders had increased DC-Treg proximity, indicating spatial immune organization as a predictor of response.

- Responders displayed reduced proximity between dendritic cells and regulatory T cells
- Non-responders showed increased dendritic cell-regulatory T cell proximity

**Scientific significance**

This study identifies spatial immune organization as a key determinant of response to CD40 agonist therapy in bladder cancer, providing a mechanistic basis for predicting clinical outcomes and guiding future immunotherapeutic strategies.

## Supramolecular Stabilization of Wide-Bandgap Perovskites Enables Durable Perovskite-Organic Tandem Solar Cells.

PMID: 42957576 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42957576/

**Summary**

This study investigates the long-term stability challenges in perovskite-organic tandem solar cells (PO-TSCs) caused by halide phase segregation in wide-bandgap perovskite subcells. Using systematic aging and mechanistic analyses, the research identifies photothermal stress-induced halide phase segregation as the primary degradation pathway. The authors develop a halogenated cyclodextrin-based supramolecular strategy that stabilizes halide homogeneity through synergistic interactions, achieving 20.60% efficiency in 1.80-eV devices with T80 lifetimes of 1100 h under MPPT and T85 lifetimes of 1128 h under 85°C thermal stress.

**Key findings**

- Halide phase segregation in wide-bandgap perovskites under photothermal stress is the dominant degradation mechanism in PO-TSCs
- A halogenated cyclodextrin supramolecular strategy enables precise regulation of crystallization, defect passivation, and halide distribution
- The approach achieves 20.60% efficiency in 1.80-eV devices with T80 lifetimes of 1100 h under MPPT and T85 lifetimes of 1128 h under 85°C thermal stress

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes a supramolecular design strategy for stabilizing wide-bandgap perovskites in tandem solar cells, addressing a critical stability bottleneck while maintaining high efficiency and flexibility.

## Lactate-induced histone lactylation drives PRMT5-mediated Foxp3 symmetric dimethylation to promote Treg differentiation and sepsis immunopathology.

PMID: 43043756 | Journal: Cell reports
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43043756/

**Summary**

This study investigates lactate's role in regulatory T cell (Treg) differentiation during sepsis. Using human sepsis patient data and murine models, researchers found plasma lactate correlates with Treg frequencies and disease severity. Lactate uptake via MCT1 increases histone lactylation and PRMT5 expression, driving symmetric dimethylation of Foxp3 at R51/R146. This stabilizes Foxp3, promoting Treg differentiation and suppressing immune responses, which exacerbates organ injury in sepsis. Targeting PRMT5 with dexmedetomidine reduced Treg accumulation and improved outcomes.

**Key findings**

- Plasma lactate levels correlate with Treg frequencies and sepsis severity in patients
- Lactate uptake via MCT1 enhances histone lactylation and PRMT5-mediated Foxp3 symmetric dimethylation
- PRMT5 inhibition with dexmedetomidine reduces Treg accumulation and improves sepsis outcomes

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes lactate metabolism as a critical regulator of Treg differentiation through epigenetic mechanisms, providing a mechanistic link between metabolic dysregulation and immunosuppression in sepsis.

## Tracing the spread of Celtic languages using ancient genomics.

PMID: 42983081 | Journal: Nature
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42983081/

**Summary**

This study investigates the demographic processes underlying the spread of Celtic languages during the Iron Age using ancient genomic data from 4,587 individuals across Bronze and Iron Age Europe. By analyzing Steppe-related ancestry migration, a southwestern European to British movement, and a westward expansion from eastern central Europe, the research identifies three key population shifts relevant to Celtic dispersal. The findings suggest that the eastern central European migration (3200-2800 BP) most parsimoniously explains Celtic language spread, though linguistic and genetic histories may not align directly.

**Key findings**

- Steppe-related ancestry arrived via Bell Beaker groups around 4400 BP across Celtic-speaking regions
- Bronze Age migration from southwestern Europe to Britain occurred between 3800-3200 BP
- Westward expansion of eastern central European ancestry (Knovíz-Urnfield) affected France, the British Isles, northeastern Iberia, and Italy from 3200-2800 BP

**Scientific significance**

This work provides genomic evidence for the most parsimonious demographic pathway in Celtic language dispersal across prehistoric Europe, clarifying the interplay between population movements and linguistic expansion.

PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42960604/

**Key findings**

- The platform achieves 75% higher signal contrast than single-channel detection under dynamic environmental conditions
- It enables continuous H2O2 monitoring despite leaf motion and fluctuating illumination, supporting field-deployable stress response assessment

**Scientific significance**

This work advances interference-resistant optical sensing for plant stress monitoring, providing a scalable method for real-time H2O2 detection in natural environments without compromising signal accuracy.

## Multi-Hydrogen Adsorption as a Dynamic Electronic Gate for Ultrafast Interfacial Charge Transfer.

PMID: 42961030 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42961030/

**Summary**

This study investigates how cooperative multi-hydrogen adsorption dynamically modulates interfacial charge transfer in catalytic systems. Using a sulfur-rich CoReS2+x cocatalyst, the researchers demonstrated that abundant unsaturated sulfur sites facilitate multi-hydrogen adsorption, inducing electron delocalization and creating an electron-deficient inter. This adsorption-driven electronic redistribution reverses electron injection thermodynamics from endothermic to spontaneous, accelerating charge transfer to ultrafast timescales. The system achieves 67.8% apparent quantum efficiency for hydrogen evolution from formic acid.

**Key findings**

- Cooperative multi-hydrogen adsorption on sulfur-rich CoReS2+x cocatalyst enables dynamic electronic gating
- Adsorption-induced electron delocalization creates an electron-deficient interfacial state
- The gated interface converts endothermic electron injection into spontaneous transfer with ultrafast kinetics

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes multi-adsorbate-induced electronic gating as a general mechanism for dynamically programming interfacial charge transfer in catalytic and energy-conversion materials under reactive conditions.

PMID: 42963326 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42963326/

**Summary**

This study investigates stabilizing cobalt-free ultrahigh-nickel layered cathodes by thermodynamically guiding antisite ordering. The synthesized cathode, LiNi0.948Al0.045Te0.007O2, exhibits ordered domains that enhance structural stability, suppress phase transitions, and maintain high capacity retention (96.7% after 200 cycles at 1 C) and Li+ transport. The approach demonstrates defect spatial organization as a key strategy to balance stability and electrochemical performance.

**Key findings**

- Tellurium doping induces spatially correlated Li/Ni antisite configurations in cobalt-free ultrahigh-nickel cathodes
- Ordered domains persist during cycling, suppressing phase separation and c-axis contraction
- The cathode maintains 96.7% capacity retention after 200 cycles at 1 C with a 4.4 V cutoff

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes defect spatial organization as a critical design principle for stabilizing high-nickel cathodes without cobalt, addressing the stability-transport trade-off through thermodynamic control of antisite configurations.
