## Thickness-Defined Transport Regime Toward Near-Ideal Area-Type Switching.

PMID: 42876208 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42876208/

**Summary**

This study investigates how ultrathin oxide memristors achieve near-ideal area-type switching by suppressing parasitic charge effects. Using TiN/Ta2O5/Pt layers (3-10 nm), researchers identified a 5-6 nm thickness window where tunneling-dominated transport minimizes readout drift and non-pinched hysteresis while maintaining analog switching. The findings reveal trap-assisted tunneling governs stabilized states, and ionic redistribution affects state reconstruction, enabling robust multi-state memristors for in-memory computing.

**Key findings**

- Ultrathin TiN/Ta2O5/Pt layers (5-6 nm) suppress non-pinched hysteresis and short-time drift
- Trap-assisted tunneling dominates stabilized-state response in near-ideal switching
- Ionic redistribution governs pristine-state reconstruction and stability boundaries

**Study type**

Device characterization and modeling

**Scientific significance**

The study establishes ultrathin scaling as a fundamental design principle for achieving near-ideal area-type memristors, enabling robust multi-state operation with minimal parasitic effects for in-memory computing applications.

- Subchondral regions feature lyso-ph:lipids associated with biomineralization genes

**Study type**

Spatial lipidomic and transcriptomic analysis

**Scientific significance**

The study establishes a spatial lipid architecture in cartilage that correlates with functional gene expression, providing a material basis for biomimetic cartilage engineering strategies.

## Contrasting demographic impacts of Roman colonization and early medieval migrations in the Iberian Peninsula.

PMID: 42906537 | Journal: Cell
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42906537/

**Summary**

This study investigates the demographic impacts of Roman colonization and early medieval migrations on the Iberian Peninsula using ancient DNA from 243 individuals spanning 100-80. Genetic analysis reveals significant Eastern Mediterranean and North African ancestry during Roman rule, followed by Germanic and Alan influences post-5th century. The Hispano-Roman population maintained continuity despite political shifts, contrasting with Britain and the central Mediterranean where demographic changes were more pronounced.

**Key findings**

- Roman period: influx of Eastern Mediterranean and North African ancestry, particularly in central and southern Iberia
- Early medieval period: Germanic and Alan (Sarmatian) groups gained political control starting in the 5th century
- Hispano-Roman population maintained demographic continuity across Iberia despite political transitions

**Study type**

Ancient DNA analysis

**Scientific significance**

The findings demonstrate regional variation in demographic continuity and admixture patterns across post-Roman Europe, highlighting how political transitions affected population structure differently than in other parts of Europe.

## Solvation-Equilibrium Locking Enables Ultrawide-Temperature Operation of High-Voltage Lithium-Metal Batteries.

PMID: 42876209 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42876209/

**Summary**

This study investigates enhancing high-voltage lithium-metal batteries' temperature resilience by designing an electrolyte with solvation-equilibrium locking. Using dimethyl sulfite and Pyr13DFOB, the researchers created an electrolyte that maintains stable ion solvation structures from -60°C to 60°C, preventing thermal degradation. The resulting batteries operate effectively at extreme temperatures, including -85°C and 120°C, with high energy density under lean conditions.

**Key findings**

- Solvation-equilibrium-locked electrolyte maintains stable solvent-separated ion pairs and contact ion pairs across a wide temperature range (-60°C to 60°C).
- The electrolyte reduces solvent coordination around Li+, minimizing interfacial decomposition and gas evolution at extreme temperatures.
- Li||NMC811 cells achieve discharge capacity at -85°C and operate up to 120°C, with pouch cells remaining functional down to -122.9°C.

**Study type**

Electrolyte design with molecular dynamics simulations and in situ NMR/Raman analysis

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes a molecular design strategy for lithium-metal batteries that enables ultrawide-temperature operation without compromising electrochemical stability, advancing the practical deployment of high-energy batteries in extreme environments.

## Dual Twisted Interfaces Enable Tunable Second Harmonic Generation in Chemical Vapor Deposition Grown Trilayer MoS2.

PMID: 42876215 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42876215/

**Summary**

This study investigates how dual twisted interfaces in CVD-grown trilayer MoS2 affect second harmonic generation (SHG). By fabricating nine distinct stacking configurations, the researchers analyzed Raman and photoluminescence data to show interface-dependent phonon and excitonic responses. Atomic-resolution imaging confirmed dual twisted interfaces and a supermoiré lattice. They developed a framework linking dual interface geometry to SHG intensity and polarization, demonstrating TT-MoS2's potential for tunable nonlinear optical engineering.

**Key findings**

- CVD-grown trilayer MoS2 with dual twisted interfaces exhibits stacking-dependent phonon and excitonic responses
- Atomic-resolution techniques confirmed dual twisted interfaces and supermoiré lattice formation
- A framework linking dual interface geometry to SHG intensity and polarization was established

**Study type**

Chemical vapor deposition (CVD) grown trilayer MoS2 with nine distinct stacking configurations

**Scientific significance**

This work provides a foundational framework for engineering tunable nonlinear optical responses in van der Waals materials through multi-interface twisted stacking.

## Directional Confinement Governed Ion Transport for Efficient Actuation.

PMID: 42876332 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42876332/

**Summary**

This study investigates how structural anisotropy in electrolytes improves ion transport for ionic actuators. By fabricating anisotropic electrolytes with oriented channels via directional freeze-drying, the researchers achieved twice the through-plane ion mobility compared to disordered electrolytes. This enhanced ion transport led to better actuator performance while balancing strain and blocking force, with effects observed across multiple polymer systems.

**Key findings**

- Anisotropic electrolytes fabricated via directional freeze-drying exhibit twice the through-plane ion mobility of disordered electrolytes
- Directional confinement significantly enhances ionic act. performance by mitigating strain-blocking force trade-offs
- The effect is reproducible across multiple polymer systems, underscoring structural anisotropy's role in ion transport regulation

**Study type**

Fabrication of anisotropic electrolytes with oriented channels via directional freeze-drying

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes structural anisotropy as a critical design principle for optimizing ion transport in electrolytes, advancing the understanding of how microstructural features govern electrochemical actuator performance beyond conventional random porous systems.

## MCT6 is an intestinal Lac-Phe exporter required for metformin-associated weight loss.

- MCT6 is an intestinal transporter specifically required for Lac-Phe efflux

- Exogenous Lac-Phe restores body weight in MCT6-knockout mice

**Study type**

Mouse genetic knockout models

**Scientific significance**

## Metazoan lagging-strand DNA synthesis is distributive.

PMID: 42913629 | Journal: Cell
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42913629/

**Summary**

This study investigates how lagging-strand DNA synthesis is coordinated with leading-strand synthesis in eukaryotes using single-molecule imaging in Xenopus extracts. It found that neither Pol α nor Pol δ is stably tethered to the replisome, revealing distributive lagging-strand synthesis where new polymerase molecules are rapidly recruited per Okazaki fragment. This indicates leading- and lagging-strand synthesis are not intrinsically coupled, resolving the topological challenge through dynamic polymerase recruitment.

**Key findings**

- Lagging-strand synthesis occurs distributively with rapid recruitment of Pol α and Pol δ for each Okazaki fragment
- Neither Pol α nor Pol δ is stably tethered to the replisome
- Leading- and lagging-strand synthesis are not intrinsically coupled in metazoans

**Study type**

Single-molecule imaging in Xenopus nuclear extracts

**Scientific significance**

The findings demonstrate that metazoans resolve anti-parallel DNA replication topology through dynamic polymerase recruitment rather than intrinsic coupling of leading- and lagging-strand synthesis.

- Taurine efflux from macrophages is triggered by NLRP3 activators, leading to inflammasome activation

In vivo mouse models and human adipose tissue analysis
