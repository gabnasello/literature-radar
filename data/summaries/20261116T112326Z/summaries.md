## Decoupled self-assembled monolayer and perovskite deposition for efficient flexible solar modules.

PMID: 42912142 | Journal: Science
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42912142/

**Summary**

This study addresses reduced power conversion efficiency and stability in flexible perovskite solar modules due to surface hydroxyls disrupting self-assembled monolayer anchoring. By decoupling SAM-perovskite deposition, the researchers accelerated SAM anchoring and improved perov. The certified PCE reached 20.2% on 750 cm² devices, with 22.0% efficiency on smaller devices showing 8% degradation after 1044 hours at 85°C and 65% humidity.

**Key findings**

- Decoupled SAM-perovskite codeposition strategy accelerates SAM anchoring by reducing SAM-perovskite interactions
- Certified power conversion efficiency of 20.2% achieved on 750 cm² flexible perovskite solar modules
- 22.0% efficient devices retained 80% of initial efficiency after 1044 hours under 85°C and 65% humidity

**Study type**

Decoupled SAM-perovskite codeposition strategy

**Scientific significance**

This approach enhances the stability and efficiency of flexible perovskite solar modules by mitigating surface hydroxyl-induced degradation, providing a pathway to commercialize high-performance portable photovoltaic devices.

- Spin-polarized polariton currents propagating up to 92 ± 1 μm at room temperature

**Study type**

Exciton-polariton waveguide

**Scientific significance**
