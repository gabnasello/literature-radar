## Implanted and degradable hydrogel evaporative cooler for regional and repeated mild hypothermia therapy of traumatic brain injury.

PMID: 42887234 | Journal: Biomaterials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42887234/

**Summary**

This study develops an implantable, degradable hydrogel evaporative cooler for regional mild hypothermia therapy in traumatic brain injury (TBI). The device, fabricated from biocompatible hydrogels, enables precise temperature regulation (33 ± 0.5 °C) during repeated 4-hour sessions over 3 days in rat models. In vivo results show neuroprotection, enhanced neural repair markers (Nestin+/Ki67+, Sox2+/Ki67+), reduced lesion volume, preserved axonal integrity, and improved cognitive function at 28 days. Proteomic data indicate hypothermia modulates synaptic plasticity, neuroinflammation, and energy metabolism.

**Key findings**

- Implantable hydrogel evaporative cooler achieves precise regional hypothermia (33 ± 00.5 °C) for 4 hours daily over 3 days in rat TBI models
- Treatment increased Nestin+/Ki67+ and Sox2+/Ki67+ progenitor-like cells, indicating neural repair
- 28-day outcomes included reduced lesion volume, preserved axonal integrity, myelination, and improved cognition
