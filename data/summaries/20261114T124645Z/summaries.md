## A microbiome-bile acid axis mediates protection from chronic liver disease in females.

PMID: 42896552 | Journal: Cell
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42896552/

**Summary**

This study investigates the mechanism behind sex-based differences in chronic liver disease (CLD) using metabolomics data from 9,254 individuals. It identifies isoursodeoxycholic acid (isoUDCA) as a bile acid mediator of female hepatoprotection, originating from a bacterial consortium enriched by estrogen. The findings suggest a microbiome-bile acid-liver axis that could explain sex disparities in CL. The study implies isoUDCA's potential as a therapeutic target.

**Key findings**

- IsoUDCA, an endogenous bile acid, mediates female hepatoprotection in chronic liver disease.
- Estrogen selectively enriches a bacterial consortium that produces isoUDCA.
- IsoUDCA promotes SREBP2 nuclear accumulation, enhancing cholesterol handling via STARD4 and CYP7B1.

**Study type**

Metabolomics analysis of large-scale human data

**Scientific significance**

The study establishes a microbiome-bile acid axis that explains sex-based differences in chronic liver disease susceptibility, providing a mechanistic basis for potential sex-specific therapeutic strategies.

**Study type**

Genome-wide analysis of archaeological samples

**Scientific significance**
