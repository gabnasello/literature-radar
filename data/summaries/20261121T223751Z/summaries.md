## Electromigration-Biased Carbon Transport and Isotope Fractionation in High-Carbon-Solubility Metals.

PMID: 42947641 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42947641/

**Summary**

This study investigates electromigration-driven carbon transport in cobalt and nickel, revealing direct current-induced graphite precipitation, significant isotope fractionation (12C enrichment up to ∼88 at%  13C), and composition-dependent thermal properties. Raman and ToF-SIMS analyses confirm isotope-sensitive transport mechanisms with enrichment factors of 1.29-1.62. The findings demonstrate current-directed material formation with isotope fractionation and thermal property variations.

**Key findings**

- Direct current drives interstitial carbon transport in cobalt and nickel, leading to graphite precipitation 8-10 mm from the cathode
- Raman and ToF-SIMS show 12C enrichment up to ∼88 at% 13C with enrichment factors of 1.29-1.62
- Composition-dependent thermal conductivity variations observed via frequency-domain thermoreflectance

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes electromigration as a mechanism for isotope-selective material formation in metals, providing insights into current-directed transport and fractionation processes relevant to understanding interstitial solute dynamics.

## Decoding the regulatory programs governing glioblastoma plasticity.

PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42983099/

**Summary**

This study investigates glioblastoma cellular plasticity using scDORI, a deep-learning framework analyzing single-cell multi-omic data from over a million cells. It reveals a structured regulatory program where asymmetric activator-repressor interactions govern state transitions. Low-plasticity states, like neuronal-like configurations, are actively maintained by safeguard repressors, with MYT1L identified as a key regulator that suppresses alternative fates and enhances therapy response.

**Key findings**

- scDORI infers enhancer-driven gene-regulatory networks at single-cell resolution in glioblastoma
- MYT1L acts as a safeguard repressor maintaining neuronal-like states by silencing alternative fates
- MYT1L restriction reduces tumor growth and invasion while loss accelerates malignant progression

**Scientific significance**

The study establishes that cellular plasticity in glioblastoma is governed by a dynamic balance of activators and repressors, highlighting safeguard repressors as potential therapeutic targets for cancers with therapy-resistant phenotypic switching.
