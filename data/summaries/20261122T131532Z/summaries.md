## Validation of clinical and preclinical obesity across global cohorts.

PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43015459/

**Summary**

This study evaluates the Lancet Commission's obesity classification framework against BMI-only methods using data from 844,108 adults across four global cohorts. It found BMI overestimates obesity in 1.2-3.1% and underestimates it in 5.2-8.6% of participants. The combined anthropometric definition increased obesity prevalence by 3.3-7.4% and showed clinical obesity (22.7-31.3% of total) carries significantly higher mortality risk than preclinical obesity.

**Key findings**

- BMI overestimates obesity prevalence by 1.2-3.1% and underestimates it by 5.2-8.6% across cohorts
- Combined anthropometric definition increased obesity prevalence by 3.3-7.4% relative to BMI-only classification
- Clinical obesity confers a hazard ratio of 1.69 for all-cause mortality versus 1.19 for preclinical obesity

**Scientific significance**

The study provides robust evidence supporting a more precise obesity classification system that better identifies high-risk individuals and improves mortality risk stratification globally.
