## A Retinal Matrix Stiffening Model Reveals FAK-Dependent Mechanotransduction in Pericytes Underlies Vascular Destabilization.

PMID: 42973071 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42973071/

**Summary**

This study investigated mechanotransduction in pericytes during diabetic retinopathy using an iPSC-derived microphysiological system. The model replicated retinal matrix stiffening observed in diabetes, revealing that progressive matrix stiffening alone induces vascular destabilization by suppressing pericyte signaling molecules via FAK-dependent mechanisms. Inhibition of FAK restored vascular integrity without hyperglycemia, suggesting FAK-dependent mechanotransduction as a druggable target for preventing diabetic retage pathogenesis.

**Key findings**

- Progressive matrix stiffening alone induces vascular defects and pericyte signaling suppression
- FAK-dependent mechanotransduction mediates pericyte signaling loss in stiffened conditions
- FAK inhibition restores vascular integrity without hyperglyemic exposure

**Scientific significance**

This work identifies matrix stiffening as a key regulator of pericyte-mediated vascular stabilization in diabetic retinopathy, establishing FAK-dependent mechanotransduction as a druggable target for therapeutic intervention.

## Enhancing TCR-T cell responses with a TGFβ-specific chimeric antigen receptor.

PMID: 43051094 | Journal: Neoplasia (New York, N.Y.)
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43051094/

**Summary**

This study investigated enhancing TCR-T cell responses in suppressive tumor microenvironments by targeting TGFβ. Researchers engineered TGFβ-specific chimeric antigen receptors (CARs) coexpressed with an HPV16 E7-targeted TCR. The TGFβ-CARs improved proliferation, cytokine production, and cytotoxicity against HPV16+ tumor cells while reducing Treg differentiation and exhaustion markers. Transcriptional changes included decreased FOXP3 and increased IFNG. In vivo, TGFβ-CAR-expressing T cells reduced tumor burden and prolonged survival in mouse models.

**Key findings**

- TGFβ-CAR coexpressing T cells showed enhanced proliferation and cytokine production against HPV16+ tumor cells
- TGFβ-CAR-expressing T cells exhibited reduced exhaustion markers and Treg differentiation after repeated antigen exposure
- In vivo, TGFβ-CAR-expressing T cells reduced tumor burden and prolonged progression-free survival in B16-F10 tumor models

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes TGFβ-targeting CARs as a strategy to counteract immunosuppressive TGFβ signaling in the tumor microenvironment, potentially improving TCR-T cell persistence and efficacy in suppressive settings.

PMID: 42973073 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42973073/

**Summary**

This study investigates quasi-continuous monitoring of urinary oxalate dynamics using a home-based multimodal sensing platform integrated into standard urine collection buckets. The platform combines electrochemical oxalate sensing with pH and volume measurements to correct matrix drift during sequential urination. It enables 24-hour monitoring under dietary hyperoxaluria, revealing distinct metabolic responses to oxalate loads and providing real-time insights into kidney stone risk. The approach offers immediate feedback for hyperoxaluria management.

**Key findings**

- Quasi-continuous 24-hour urinary oxalate monitoring via integrated electrochemical sensing
- Correction of matrix drift using simultaneous pH and volume measurements
- Distinct metabolic response profiles under different dietary oxalate loads

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes a practical method for real-time oxalate metabolism assessment in hyperoxaluria, advancing decentralized metabolic monitoring strategies for kidney stone risk management.

## Attribution Fingerprinting by Interpretable Machine Learning Reveals Category-Specific Additive Design Rules for Zinc Anodes.

PMID: 42973079 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42973079/

**Summary**

This study investigates how molecular descriptors and machine learning can predict cycle life in aqueous zinc-ion batteries using interpretable methods. By clustering additives into three categories based on SHAP profiles, the research identifies design rules tied to adsorption energetics, charge-transfer gaps, and hydrogen-bond networks. Predictions for novel additives align closely with experimental results, demonstrating that additive performance depends on specific structural features rather than a single universal descriptor. The approach provides a framework for rational design of stable zinc anodes.

**Key findings**

- Additives cluster into three categories governed by adsorption energetics, charge-transfer gaps, and hydrogen-bond network regulation
- Predicted cycle life for 2-fluoropyridine-5-carbaldehyde (2FPD) matches experimental data within 6.5% (3218 h vs. 3010 h)
- Structural features like aldehydes and fluorine atoms significantly enhance cycle life, with aldehydes alone improving performance from 89 h to 900 h

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes category-specific design rules for zinc anode additives, moving beyond empirical screening to enable interpretable, structure-guided optimization of aqueous zinc-ion battery stability.

**Key findings**

- PSC EVs contain unique pluripotency markers including ROR1, CD133, and miR-302 family members
- Local injections of induced PSC EVs increased diabetes-free survival by boosting regulatory T cells and preserving β-cell mass in a type 1 diabetes model

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes pluripotent stem cell-derived extracellular vesicles as a scalable platform for systemic immune modulation in autoimmune diseases, offering a novel approach to induce immune tolerance without requiring cell transplantation.

## Cross-System Recycling of Spent Layered Oxides Boosts Synergistic Optimization of Na4Fe3(PO4)2P2O7 Cathodes via Site-Selective Ni/Mn Co-Doping.

PMID: 42973093 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42973093/

**Summary**

This study investigates cross-system recycling of spent sodium-ion battery cathodes to enhance Na4Fe3(PO4)2P2.9O7 cathodes. By leaching nickel and manganese from degraded layered oxides (NaNi1/3Fe1/3Mn1/3O2) and co-doping them into the polyanionic cathode, the researchers achieved site-selective optimization. **Key findings**

- Site-selective Ni/Mn co-doping from recycled layered oxides enhances Na4Fe3(PO4)2P2O7 cathode performance
- The optimized cathode achieves 91.2% purity, 92.84% initial coulombic efficiency, and 86.74% capacity retention after 12,000 cycles
- NFNMPP//HC pouch cells demonstrate 5557 cycles with 82.37% capacity retention at 30 C and a wide operating temperature range (-40°C to 55°C)

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes a cross-system recycling strategy to transform spent sodium-ion battery cathodes into high-performance polyanionic materials, advancing sustainable cathode optimization for energy storage applications.

- H2 permeance of 3896 ± 107 GPU and H2/CO2 separation factor of 88.5 ± 5.9

**Summary**

The model, trained on TCGA data with feature selection via mRMR and RFE, generated a Pathomics Score (PS) that correlated with FOXP3+ cell density and was independently associated with poor overall survival (HR = 1.68, p = 0.008). The PS also identified immune activation pathways and immunosuppressive features in the tumor microenvironment.

**Key findings**

- High nTreg infiltration predicted poor overall survival with a hazard ratio of 1.68 (95% CI 1.15-2.46, p = 0.008)
- The Pathomics Score (PS) achieved an AUC-ROC of 0.72 in validation cohort and strongly correlated with FOXP3+ cell density (p < 0.001)
- High PS patients exhibited immune activation pathways and immunosuppressive microenvironment features including upregulated immune checkpoint genes

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes a non-invasive digital biomarker derived from standard histopathology images that quantifies immunosuppressive regulatory T cell infiltration and its prognostic impact in breast cancer, advancing the field of computational pathomics.
