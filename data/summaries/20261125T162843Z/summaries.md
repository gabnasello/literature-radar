## Breaking the Capacity-Reversibility Linear Trade-Off in Li-O2 Batteries via Amorphous Non-Precious High-Entropy Alloy Catalysts.

PMID: 42992419 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42992419/

**Summary**

This study investigates breaking the capacity-reversibility trade-off in Li-O2 batteries using amorphous non-precious MnFeCoNiCu high-entropy alloy (A-HEA) catalysts. The researchers designed A-HEA via liquid-phase reduction to create a porous structure enhancing electrochemical surface area and promoting solution-mediated discharge pathways. The catalyst achieved 10.8 mAh cm-2 capacity with reduced overpotential (0.75 V) and extended cycle life 4-5 times versus crystalline analogs. The material demonstrated scalability and performance in pouch cells at high rates and low temperatures.

**Key findings**

- A-HEA catalyst achieved 10.8 mAh cm-2 discharge capacity
- Reduced overpotential to 0.75 V and 4-5x longer cycle life versus crystalline catalysts
- Scalable production and high-performance in pouch cells at 400 mA and -30°C

**Scientific significance**

This work demonstrates a non-precious catalyst strategy to overcome the fundamental capacity-reversibility trade-off in Li-O2 batteries, enabling higher energy density and longer cycle life without compromising reversibility.

- NLGP suppressed Treg-associated genes (IL10 and FOXP3) indicating reduced regulatory T cell function

**Scientific significance**

NLGP demonstrates a mechanism for enhancing antitumor immunity in NSCLC by modulating T cell differentiation toward a tumoricidal TH1 response while inhibiting immunosuppressive regulatory T cells.

## Spin-Selective Hydrogen Utilization Enables Efficient Electrochemical CO2-to-CH4 Conversion in Ag-Modified Chiral Cu Catalysts for Unassisted Solar Fuel Production.

PMID: 42992798 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42992798/

**Summary**

This study investigates spin-selective hydrogen utilization in Ag-modified chiral Cu electrocatalysts for efficient CO2-to-CH4 conversion. The researchers leveraged structural chirality to induce chirality-induced spin selectivity, which suppresses spin-singlet H2 formation and mitigates the competing hydrogen evolution reaction. Ag modification enhanced *CO intermediate supply, enabling a 67.0% CH4 Farada. The catalyst achieved unassisted solar-driven CO2-to-CH4 conversion with 54.1% CH4 FE and 5.86% solar-to-fuel efficiency.

**Key findings**

- Chiral Cu frameworks with Ag modification suppress HER by favoring spin-selective hydrogen utilization
- The catalyst achieves 67.0% CH4 Faradaic efficiency at -0.7 V vs. RHE
- Unassisted solar-driven CO2-to-CH4 conversion yields 54.1% CH4 FE and 5.86% solar-to-fuel efficiency

**Scientific significance**

This work demonstrates spin-selective hydrogen utilization as a viable strategy for selective hydrocarbon synthesis and bias-free solar CO2-to-fuel conversion.

## Causal associations between immune cell traits and hip osteoarthritis: A bidirectional two-sample Mendelian randomization study.

PMID: 43063075 | Journal: Medicine
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43063075/

**Summary**

This study used bidirectional two-sample Mendelian randomization to investigate causal relationships between 731 immune cell traits and hip osteoarthritis (HOA) risk. Analysis identified 17 immune traits with statistically significant associations (FDR < 0.05) in the forward direction, including six CD33-expressing myeloid traits positively associated with HOA risk and eleven lymphoid/monocyte traits inversely associated. Reverse analysis showed no robust evidence for HOA causing immune changes. Findings suggest potential immune-mediated HOA mechanisms but require experimental validation.

**Key findings**

- Six CD33-expressing myeloid cell traits showed positive associations with HOA risk (odds ratio 1.02, 95% CI 1.01-1.03)
- Eleven immune traits including T cells, B cells, NK cells, regulatory T cells, and dendritic cells showed inverse associations with HOA risk (odds ratio range 0.90-0.97)
- Reverse MR analysis found no robust evidence for causal effects of HOA on immune cell composition after FDR correction

**Scientific significance**

This study provides genetic evidence for immune cell traits influencing hip osteoarthritis risk through Mendelian randomization, advancing understanding of potential immunological pathways in osteoarthritis pathophysiology.

## Coupling Iodine Conversion and Zinc Ion Transport via Molecular Catalysis for Long-Life Zinc-Iodine Batteries.

PMID: 42992839 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42992839/

**Summary**

This study investigates enhancing zinc-iodine battery performance by resolving polyiodide shuttling and interfacial mass-transport issues. Using molecular catalyst screening, iron(III) meso-tetrakis(4-carboxyphenyl) porphyrin (Fe-TCPP) was identified as an interfacial regulator that stabilizes iodide intermediates without passivating the interface and promotes zinc ion accessibility. The Fe-TCPP-regulated cathode achieves 178 mAh g-1 capacity and 50,000 cycles at 5 A g-1, demonstrating effective synchronization of electron transfer and ion redistribution for long-term stability.

**Key findings**

- Fe-TCPP stabilizes iodide intermediates without interfacial passivation
- Peripheral carboxyphenyl groups enhance zinc ion accessibility
- The cathode achieves 178 mAh g-1 capacity and 50,000 cycles at 5 A g-1

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes a molecular-catalytic strategy for simultaneously regulating redox-intermediate conversion and ion transport in aqueous zinc-iodine batteries, addressing a critical bottleneck for practical implementation.

## Quantum Saturation of the Electro-Optic Effect.

PMID: 43000607 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43000607/

**Summary**

This study investigates quantum effects in electro-optic materials at cryogenic temperatures. By combining thermodynamic analysis, phase-field simulations, experimental growth, and cryogenic optical measurements, researchers demonstrated that quantum fluctuations can stabilize a large electro-optic response (reff = 2850 pm/V) near 0 K. The approach tuned ferroelectric phase boundaries to achieve temperature independence below 25 K, outperforming room-temperature and cryogenic benchmarks by orders of magnitude.

**Key findings**

- Quantum fluctuations enable temperature-independent electro-optic response below 25 K
- BaTiO3 on GdScO3 achieves reff = 2850 pm/V at cryogenic temperatures
- Ba0.77Ca0.23TiO3 shows r51 = 1898 pm/V, surpassing BaTiO3-on-Si by over an order of magnitude

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes a quantum design principle for cryogenic electro-optic materials, overcoming traditional temperature sensitivity limitations through engineered phase boundaries.

PMID: 43001351 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43001351/

**Summary**

This study addresses the intrinsic inefficiency of Fe single-atom catalysts (Fe-N4) by engineering carbon defects adjacent to Fe sites using bismuth-mediated zinc extraction from ZIF-8. The Fe-N-pCd catalyst achieved a high half-wave potential of 0.918 V vs. RHE in alkaline electrolytes and demonstrated stable Zn-air battery operation beyond 3000 hours at -40°C. Defects redistributed electron density, favoring an axial *OH-bound Fe state and accelerating oxygen reduction kinetics by altering the rate-limiting step.

**Key findings**

- Bismuth-mediated zinc extraction creates carbon defects adjacent to Fe-N4 sites on ZIF-8-derived carbon
- Fe-N-pCd catalyst achieved 0.918 V half-wave potential vs. RHE in alkaline electrolytes
- Quasi-solid-state Zn-air battery with Fe-N-pCd cathode operated stably for over 3000 hours at -40°C

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes a defect engineering strategy to optimize single-atom catalyst microenvironments, enhancing oxygen reduction kinetics by modifying electronic structure and reaction pathways.

**Scientific significance**

PMID: 43001411 | Journal: Advanced Materials

**Summary**

This study investigates enhancing terahertz hyperspectral imaging using high-density pixelated metasurfaces. The approach leverages photonic crystal heterojunctions to create pixelated metasurfaces that encode spatial information into spectrally resolved resonances. Key results include suppressing lateral leakage, enabling three-dimensional photon confinement, and achieving nine independently addressable resonances in a single 3×3 pixel array. **Key findings**

- Pixelated metasurfaces with ∼2λ lateral dimensions retain high spectral fidelity and signal-to-noise ratio
- Three-dimensional photon confinement enables enhanced quality factors without increasing device footprint
- Single-shot broadband terahertz measurement retrieves nine independently addressable resonances

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes a direct spectral-spatial encoding mechanism for terahertz hyperspectral imaging, overcoming traditional limitations in spatial resolution, spectral sampling, and computational requirements.
