## The blood-brain barrier imports diverse neuroprotective proteins through pathways impaired early in Alzheimer's disease models.

PMID: 43014307 | Journal: Cell
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43014307/

**Summary**

This study investigates blood-brain barrier protein transport mechanisms using bioorthogonal proteomics. It identifies 191 plasma proteins sampled and 92 transported into the brain, enriched for carrier functions. The findings reveal cell-type specific protein tropism, with APOA1 targeting astrocytes via SR-B1 and ABCA1, a key Alzheimer's risk gene. Early Alzheimer's models show selective transport remodeling, and hepatic APOA1 overexpression rescues deficits.

**Key findings**

- The healthy blood-brain barrier samples 191 plasma proteins and permits 92 into brain parenchyma
- APOA1 crosses the BBB via SR-B1 and engages astrocytic ABCA1, a key Alzheimer's risk gene
- Early Alzheimer's models exhibit reduced APOA1 and increased SERPINA1 uptake, with hepatic APOA1 overexpression rescuing synaptic and behavioral deficits

**Study type**

Bioorthogonal proteomics

**Scientific significance**

This work redefines the blood-brain barrier as a selective interface that recruits neuroprotective circulatory proteins to maintain brain health, providing mechanistic insights into early Alzheimer's disease pathogenesis.

**Study type**

Integrated analyses of secondary lymphoid organs

**Scientific significance**

## Altermagnetic signatures and topological textures in ultrathin multiferroic BiFeO3.

PMID: 42951904 | Journal: Nature Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42951904/

**Summary**

This study investigates ultrathin BiFeO3 multiferroics to address degradation of magnetic and polar order in thin films. Using first-principles calculations, symmetry analysis, and atomic imaging, researchers identified a phase transition at four unit cells that eliminates magnetic dead layers. The ultrathin film exhibits collinear monoclinic structure with d-wave altermagnetic order, enabling stable multiferroic behavior at device-relevant thicknesses.

**Key findings**

- Ultrathin BiFeO3 films at four unit cells show no magnetic dead layers
- Collinear monoclinic phase with d-wave altermagnetic order stabilizes at this thickness
- Short-circuit electrostatic boundary conditions and epitaxial strain drive the phase transition

**Study type**

First-principles calculations, symmetry analysis, atomic-resolution imaging and magnetic imaging

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes a pathway to stabilize unconventional multiferroicity at device-relevant thicknesses, overcoming traditional scaling limits for oxide electronics.

## Thermodynamic origin of the phonon Hall effect in a honeycomb antiferromagnet.

PMID: 42912136 | Journal: Science
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42912136/

**Summary**

This study investigates the thermodynamic origin of phonon-mediated thermal Hall effects in honeycomb antiferromagnets. Researchers measured longitudinal and transverse thermal conductivities in nickel phosphorus trisulfide (NiPS3), finding peak values at the same temperature with a thermal Hall angle within a theoretical bound. The transverse conductivity strongly depends on b-axis magnetization but shows no correlation with phonon mean free path, suggesting an intrinsic mechanism.

**Key findings**

- Longitudinal and trans: thermal conductivities peak at the same temperature
- Thermal Hall angle at peak temperature respects a theoretical bound
- Transverse thermal conductivity correlates strongly with b-axis magnetization but not with phonon mean free path

**Study type**

Experimental

**Scientific significance**

This work identifies an intrinsic mechanism for phonon thermal Hall effects in honeycomb antiferromagnets, advancing understanding of thermal transport in magnetically ordered materials.

## Free-standing two-dimensional metal oxides at the single-atomic-layer limit.

PMID: 42957885 | Journal: Nature Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42957885/

**Summary**

This study addresses the challenge of synthesizing free-standing two-dimensional metal oxides at the single-atomic-layer limit. Using electron-beam-induced dehalogenation of layered metal oxyhalides, researchers fabricated CrO, ErO, and BiO monolayers exceeding 104 nm². **Key findings**

- Controlled fabrication of free-standing CrO, ErO, and BiO monolayers via electron-beam dehalogenation
- Monolayer CrO exhibits antiferromagnetic order below 230 K (predicted by DFT)
- Fabrication of metal oxide heterostructures with pronounced excitonic responses

**Study type**

Electron-beam-induced dehalogenation of layered metal oxyhalides

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes a scalable method for synthesizing single-atomic-layer metal oxides, enabling exploration of quantum phenomena and excitonic effects in ultrathin two-dimensional materials.
