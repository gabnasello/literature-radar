## Stress-Resistant Plant Cell-Wall Inspired Cell Armor Enables Red Blood Cell Lyophilization.

PMID: 42893398 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42893398/

**Summary**

This study investigates engineering a plant-inspired cell armor to enable red blood cell lyophilization. Using a biomimetic approach, the researchers developed an artificial membrane armor (AMA) combining ZIF-8 for mechanical support, a water-retentive polymer matrix, and Zn2+ coordination to maintain membrane fluidity. The AMA achieved ∼95% post-rehydration recovery with minimal protectant content and a 3-month shelf life, preserving RBC functionality without cold-chain requirements.

**Key findings**

- The biomimetic cell armor enabled ∼95% post-rehydration recovery of red blood cells after lyophilization
- The armor achieved an ultra-low protectant content (<5 wt%) and ambient shelf stability for at least 3 months
- Rehydrated red blood cells maintained native morphology, membrane mechanics, metabolic activity, and oxygen transport capability

**Study type**

Biomimetic engineering

**Scientific significance**

This work demonstrates a transferable strategy for imparting desiccation tolerance from plant cell walls to mammalian cells, enabling ambient-temperature storage of sensitive biological systems without cold-chain dependence.

**Key findings**

- Intermediate semi-graphitized carbon phase maximizes near-infrared absorption and selective two-electron oxygen reduction
- Non-monotonic activity dependence on graphitization degree with optimal performance at intermediate stages
- NIR-driven H2O2 generation enables self-amplifying redox cascade for cancer therapy without sacrificial agents

**Study type**

Photocatalytic carbon nanostructure engineering

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes an electronic-structure paradigm for carbon-based photocatalysis, enabling selective two-electron oxygen reduction under physiological conditions and providing a generalizable strategy for modulating reaction efficiency in metal-free systems.

## CRISPR mutagenesis of the MYC locus reveals a druggable ultraconserved RNA element.

PMID: 42982038 | Journal: Cell
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42982038/

**Summary**

The study used CRISPR mutagenesis to map functional elements in the MYC locus at base-pair resolution, revealing that 67% of essential base pairs are noncoding. It identified an ultraconserved noncoding RNA element in the MYC 3' UTR that regulates mRNA and protein localization via hnRNP L interaction. Steric-blocking ASOs targeting this element selectively kill MYC-dependent cancer cells and inhibit tumor growth, highlighting noncoding sequences as critical functional carriers and identifying a druggable target.

**Key findings**

- 67% of functionally essential base pairs in the MYC locus are noncoding
- An ultraconserved noncoding RNA element in the MYC 3' UTR promotes perinuclear mRNA localization and nuclear protein localization via hnRNP L interaction
- Steric-blocking ASOs targeting this RNA element selectively eliminate MYC-dependent cancer cells and inhibit patient-derived tumor organoid growth

**Study type**

CRISPR tiling mutagenesis

**Scientific significance**

This work demonstrates that noncoding sequences are major carriers of functional genetic information and identifies a druggable RNA element that disrupts MYC-driven cancer mechanisms through targeted therapeutic intervention.

## Breaking the Boltzmann Limit: A Review of Emerging Ultralow-Power Transistor Technologies.

PMID: 42894199 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42894199/

**Summary**

This review examines ultralow-power transistor technologies beyond the 60 mV/dec Boltzmann limit for sub-2 nm CMOS scaling. It analyzes tunnel field-effect transistors (TFETs), negative-capacitance field-effect transistors (NCFETs), and cold-source field-effect transistors (CSFETs), discussing their physical principles, recent advances, optimization strategies, and remaining challenges for future integration with advanced CMOS nodes.

**Key findings**

- TFETs, NCFETs, and CSFETs are the primary sub-60 mV/dec technologies addressing CMOS scaling bottlenecks
- The review details underlying physical principles and optimization strategies for these ultralow-power transistors
- Future breakthroughs require novel materials, multi-mechanism integration, and experimental verification with advanced CMOS nodes

**Scientific significance**

This review provides a comprehensive analysis of emerging ultralow-power transistor technologies that could overcome the fundamental Boltzmann thermal limit, enabling further scaling of sub-2 nm CMOS transistors through novel device physics.

## Disentangling Polaron Formation Pathways in Y6 by Probing Noninteracting and Interacting Chromophores via Transient Absorption Spectroscopy.

PMID: 42894260 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42894260/

**Summary**

This study uses transient absorption spectroscopy to distinguish between noninteracting and interacting Y6 chromophores in solution and thin films. It identifies localized Frenkel excitons (LE) in noninteracting Y6 and aggregate excitons (AE) in interacting Y6, with AEs showing fluence-dependent lifetimes due to singlet-singlet annihilation. Long-lived polaronic states form on aggregated Y6 within tens of picoseconds, persisting into the nanosecond regime. The findings suggest that polaron formation in Y6 films arises from an Onsager-like autoionization process of delocalized excitons in interacting domains.

**Key findings**

- Localized Frenkel-type excitons (LE) decay monoexponentially on nanosecond timescales in noninteracting Y6
- Aggregate excitons (AE) exhibit fluence-dependent lifetimes (30-200 ps) governed by singlet-singlet annihilation
- Long-lived polaronic states form on aggregated Y6 within tens of picoseconds and persist into the nanosecond regime

**Study type**

Transient absorption spectroscopy

**Scientific significance**

The study provides a framework for interpreting transient absorption features in Y6 and identifies the Onsager-like autoionization mechanism underlying polaron formation in interacting domains, advancing understanding of charge generation in nonfullerene acceptor photovoltaics.

## A Simple Descriptor for Solid-State Ionic Conductors Across Chemistries.

PMID: 42894264 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42894264/

**Summary**

This study investigates a simple geometric descriptor, ∆d, for predicting ion migration barriers in solid-state ionic conductors across multiple chemist: Li-, Na-, K-, and Mg-ions in oxides, sulfides, and halides. By analyzing migration barriers, ∆d captures short-range repulsion penalties and distinguishes low- from high-barrier pathways. It enables high-throughput screening of conductors and links diverse transport mechanisms to a common physical quantity.

**Key findings**

- ∆d measures the largest contraction of mobile-ion-neighbor distances relative to equilibrium values along migration pathways
- ∆d consistently distinguishes low- from high-barrier pathways across different ion chemistries and material classes
- High-throughput screening using ∆d identifies known fast-ion conductors and previously unexplored candidates validated by simulations

**Study type**

Computational screening and descriptor development

**Scientific significance**

This work provides a transferable geometric descriptor that unifies ion migration barrier prediction across diverse mobile-ion chemistries and material classes, advancing the rational design of solid-state ionic conductors.

**Study type**

Termination-resolved micro-ARPES, spin-ARPES, and density functional theory

**Scientific significance**

## Chiral Phonon-Driven Ultrafast Spin Currents in a Non-Magnetic Ferroelectric.

PMID: 42894276 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42894276/

**Summary**

This study investigates whether chiral phonons in non-magnetic ferroelectrics can drive ultrafast spin currents. Using broadband terahertz emission spectroscopy, researchers observed spin currents in In2Se3 following ultrafast laser excitation, attributing the effect to a chiral phonon-induced spin Seebeck mechanism. The results demonstrate controllable ter:ellipticity and sign reversal with sample orientation or material changes, suggesting chiral phonons enable ultrafast spin current generation without magnetic materials.

**Key findings**

- In2Se3 generates ultrafast spin currents via chiral phonon-induced spin Seebeck effect after laser excitation
- Terahertz emission shows sign reversal with sample orientation or Pt/W overlayer substitution
- Polarization-resolved measurements reveal controllable terahertz ellipticity indicating polarization-dependent ultrafast response

**Study type**

Broadband terahertz emission spectroscopy

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes chiral phonons as a mechanism for generating ultrafast spin currents in non-magnetic materials, offering a new pathway for terahertz sources with switchable handedness without magnetic components.
