## Connectome simulations identify a central pattern generator for fly walking.

PMID: 42951971 | Journal: Nature
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42951971/

**Summary**

This study used computational simulations of the Drosophila ventral nerve cord connectomes to identify a central pattern generator (CPG) for fly walking. It discovered a minimal three-neuron circuit comprising one inhibitory and two excitatory interneurons that generates rhythmic leg movements across all six legs. The circuit was necessary and sufficient for motor rhythms and was validated through optogenetic experiments.

**Key findings**

- A minimal three-neuron circuit (one inhibitory, two excitatory interneurons) generates rhythmic leg motor activity in flies
- This circuit is necessary and sufficient for coordinated rhythmic movement across all six legs
- Optogenetic validation confirmed a separate descending pathway (DNb08) drives rhythmic leg movements

**Scientific significance**

The study provides the first identified cellular and synaptic structure of a central pattern generator for walking in any animal, advancing understanding of rhythmic motor control mechanisms.
