## Closed Pores as Internal Stress-Regulation Units for Practical Silicon-Carbon Anodes.

PMID: 42895128 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42895128/

**Summary**

This study investigates closed nanopores in silicon-carbon anodes as stress-regulation units to mitigate lithiation-induced deformation and interphase degradation. By engineering heteroatom-doped carbon hosts with abundant closed pores, the material redirects expansion stress inward, reducing electrode swelling. The nitrogen-doped composite achieves 1756.5 mAh g-1 reversible capacity, 90.2% initial Coulombic efficiency, and 868.4 mAh g-1 at 3 C, while minimizing thickness expansion to 13.1% after 50 cycles.

**Key findings**

- Closed nanopores in heteroatom-doped carbon hosts redirect lithiation-induced stress inward, suppressing electrode swelling
- Nitrogen-doped silicon-carbon composites achieve 1756.5 mAh g-1 reversible capacity and 90.2% initial Coulombic efficiency
- The design reduces thickness expansion from 38.4% to 13.1% after 50 cycles, enabling scalable production for battery applications

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes closed-pore engineering as a scalable strategy for developing mechanically stable silicon-carbon anodes with minimal expansion, advancing practical lithium-ion battery electrode design.

**Summary**

This study investigates how adsorbate bonding at platinum surfaces extends beyond the adsorption site due to delocalized electronic bands. Using velocity-resolved kinetics, researchers measured bond strength changes of three molecules across 100 angstroms of step-step distance on Pt, finding variations up to 0.2 eV. The results highlight molecule-specific orbital hybridization with metal bands, influencing bond strength through structural modifications.

**Key findings**

- Bond strength variations of up to 0.2 eV observed across 100 angstroms of step-step distance on platinum
- Molecule-specific orbital hybridization with symmetry-selected metal bands
- Structural modifications of the metal surface directly impact molecule-metal bond strength

**Scientific significance**

The findings demonstrate that electronic delocalization in catalytic metal surfaces significantly influences adsorbate bond strength over large structural scales, providing insights into surface reactivity mechanisms.

## Multimodal X-ray imaging reveals hierarchical fibre mechanics.

PMID: 42983031 | Journal: Nature
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42983031/

**Summary**

This study investigates how intervertebral discs (IVDs) integrate nanoscale fibril mechanics with fibre-scale organization under load. Using TomoSAXS, a novel 3D X-ray scattering technique, researchers mapped fibril-to-fibre mechanics in intact tissue. Key results include inverse relationships between intrafibrillar pre-strain and fibril strain, lamellar texture of pre-strain, and critical roles of annulus fibrosus-nucleus pulposus interfaces in local mechanics. These findings elucidate load-sharing mechanisms maintaining IVD elasticity.

**Key findings**

- Intrafibrillar molecular pre-strain (D-period stagger) exhibits lamellar texture and correlates tightly with microscale fibre strain
- Pre-strain inversely relates to fibril strain and its variability, indicating load-sharing through molecular unwinding
- Radial strain bridges and high-curvature zones at annulus fib: fibrosus-nucleus pulposus interfaces regulate local mechanics

**Scientific significance**

TomoSAXS provides a platform for visualizing nanoscale-to-microscale matrix mechanics in fibrous materials, advancing understanding of biomechanical equilibrium in biological and engineered systems.
