By applying continuous strain near the magic angle (≈1.1°), researchers observed pronounced elastoresistance dependent on band filling and temperature. - Potential origins include nematic fluctuations and enhanced electronic entropy from fluctuating isospin moments

**Scientific significance**

**Summary**

This study investigates domain-alteration chimeras (DALTACs) as a therapeutic strategy for castration-resistant prostate cancer. By engineering synthetic interactions between the androgen receptor (AR) ligand-binding domain and p300/CBP bromodomain, DALTAC-1 reprograms oncogenic complexes, suppressing AR-driven transcription and tumor growth more effectively than dual inhibition. It induces histone modifications and disrupts AR neo-enhancers, showing lineage-specific efficacy in preclinical models.

**Key findings**

- DALTAC-1 reprograms AR-p300/CBP interactions by enforcing non-native domain proximity, locking the complex in a non-productive state
- DALTAC-1 suppresses AR-driven transcription and proliferation more potently than combined AR and p300/CBP inhibition
- DALTAC-1 induces histone H2B N-terminal deacetylation and neomorphic AR acetylation, collapsing oncogenic AR neo-enhanceosomes

**Scientific significance**

This approach demonstrates a novel mechanism to disrupt oncogenic protein complexes through engineered domain-topology alterations, offering a targeted strategy for castration-resistant prostate cancer with potential broader applications in complex-driven diseases.

**Scientific significance**
