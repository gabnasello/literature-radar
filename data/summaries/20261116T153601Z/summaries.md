## Dual Twisted Interfaces Enable Tunable Second Harmonic Generation in Chemical Vapor Deposition Grown Trilayer MoS2.

PMID: 42876215 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42876215/

**Summary**

This study investigates how dual twisted interfaces in CVD-grown trilayer MoS2 affect second harmonic generation (SHG). By fabricating nine distinct configurations, researchers analyzed Raman and photoluminescence data to show stacking-dependent phonon and excitonic responses. Atomic-resolution techniques confirmed dual twisted interfaces and a supermoiré lattice. The study developed a framework linking dual interfaces to SHG intensity and polarization, establishing TT-MoS2 as a tunable nonlinear optical platform.

**Key findings**

- Dual twisted interfaces in trilayer MoS2 create stacking-dependent phonon and excitonic responses
- Atomic-resolution techniques confirmed dual twisted interfaces and a supermoiré lattice structure
- A framework linking dual interfaces to SHG intensity and polarization was developed for engineering nonlinear optical responses

**Study type**

CVD-grown twisted trilayer MoS2 configurations

**Scientific significance**

This work provides a foundational framework for engineering multi-interface twisted coupling to tune nonlinear optical responses in van der Waals materials.

## Structural basis for protocadherin-1 recognition by New World hantaviruses.

PMID: 42907501 | Journal: Nature
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42907501/

**Summary**

This study elucidates the structural basis of New World hantavirus entry via protocadherin-1 (PCDH1) using near-atomic resolution cryo-EM. It reveals how PCDH1 asymmetrically binds a deep pocket in the viral spike, explains receptor specificity between New and Old World hantaviruses, and demonstrates antibody mechanisms to block this interaction for therapeutic design.

**Key findings**

- PCDH1 binds a deep pocket formed by four Gn protomers in the hantavirus spike
- Three Gn loops determine receptor specificity, transferring binding from New to Old World hantaviruses
- Neutralizing antibodies block PCDH1 recognition via steric hindrance or site occlusion

**Study type**

cryo-EM

**Scientific significance**

This work defines the structural mechanism of hantavirus-receptor recognition, revealing how viral spikes conceal binding sites and informing antibody-based therapeutic strategies against hantavirus infections.

## Process-intrinsic cellular network for sustained strength-ductility across temperatures in a 3D-printed multi-principal-element alloy.

PMID: 42972073 | Journal: Nature Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42972073/

**Summary**

This study investigates a 3D-printed multi-principal-element alloy (MPEA) designed to maintain strength-ductility across temperatures via an intrinsic cellular network formed during laser additive manufacturing. The alloy features a self-assembled body-centred cubic/L21 precipitate structure that enables slip transfer and back-stress hardening across specific interfaces. This mechanism stabilizes deformation from 77 to 873 K, achieving yield strengths above 1 GPa and uniform elongation of ~20% without post-processing.

**Key findings**

- A (CrFeCo)48Ni34Al7Ti5V5Ta1 MPEA with a self-assembled body-centred cubic/L21 precipitate cellular network formed intrinsically during LAM
- Slip transfer and back-stress hardening across Kurdjumov-Sachs and Nishiyama-Wassermann interfaces stabilize deformation from 77 to 873 K
- The alloy maintains yield strength >1 GPa and uniform elongation ~20% over a wide temperature range without post-treatment

**Study type**

Laser additive manufacturing (LAM) of multi-principal-element alloys

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes a process-intrinsic pathway for designing temperature-stable, high-strength alloys through interface-mediated plasticity mechanisms in additive manufacturing.

## The contribution of salt marshes to wave attenuation during extreme storms.

PMID: 42907502 | Journal: Nature
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42907502/

**Summary**

This study investigated salt marsh resilience during extreme storms using a wave flume experiment with intact marshes exposed to up to 2 m significant wave heights. Results indicate marsh substrates remain stable despite wave exposure, while vegetation progressively degrades, reducing wave attenuation. Attenuation exceeds 10% at low-energy coasts (≤1 m wave height) but remains below 10% at high-energy coasts (>1 m wave height).

**Key findings**

- Marsh substrates persist with negligible erosion under cumulative wave exposure up to 2 m significant wave height
- Vegetation degradation progressively reduces wave attenuation during prolonged wave exposure
- Wave attenuation by marsh vegetation exceeds 10% at low-energy coasts (≤1 m wave height) but remains below 10% at high-energy coasts (>1 m wave height)

**Study type**

Wave flume experiment

**Scientific significance**

The study provides critical evidence on salt marsh resilience thresholds under extreme wave events, clarifying the conditions where vegetation-driven wave attenuation remains effective for coastal protection.

Synthetic polymer network
