## Nonreciprocal Bending in Ferroelectric Membranes Driven by Flexoelectricity.

PMID: 42872324 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42872324/

**Summary**

This study investigates nonreciprocal bending in ferroelectric membranes using suspended-membrane nanoindentation. It demonstrates asymmetric bending with a 33% difference in effective elastic modulus between opposite directions and polarity-dependent elastic recovery. The findings attribute this behavior to asymmetric ferroelectric domain switching driven by flexoelectricity, highlighting a novel nonreciprocal mechanism in ferroelectrics.

**Key findings**

- Asymmetric bending in ferroelectric PbTiO3 membranes with a 3:1 elastic modulus difference between opposite directions
- Polarity-dependent elastic recovery after bending
- Nonreciprocal behavior originates from asymmetric ferroelectric domain switching via flexoelectricity

**Study type**

Suspended-membrane nanoindentation

**Scientific significance**

This work identifies flexoelectricity as a key mechanism for inducing nonreciprocal mechanical responses in ferroelectrics, expanding the understanding of unconventional mechanical behaviors in these materials.

## Cellular self-organization orchestrates ferroptosis propagation.

PMID: 42880281 | Journal: Cell
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42880281/

**Summary**

The study investigates how cellular self-organization regulates ferroptosis propagation. Using in silico and pharmacological approaches, it demonstrates that spatial patterns of cell density and alignment encode transcriptional programs, creating ferro: sensitive regions (low-density, misaligned) and propagation-resistant boundaries (high-density, aligned). These patterns constrain ferroptosis spread through polarized lipid distribution and YAP/TEAD1 or NRF2 activity.

**Key findings**

- Low-density, misaligned regions with elevated YAP/TEAD1 activity are ferroptosis-sensitive
- High-density, aligned regions with increased NRF2 expression form propagation-resistant boundaries
- Polarized lipid distribution at cell poles creates peroxidation hotspots driving anisotropic ferroptosis propagation

**Study type**

In silico and pharmacological perturbations

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes cellular self-organization as a systems-level mechanism that constrains ferroptosis propagation, offering a framework for understanding tissue-scale regulation of cell death in development and disease.

## Beyond Facet Engineering: Dislocation-Mediated Nucleation Enables Stable Lithium Deposition on Practical Polycrystalline Cu Collectors.

PMID: 42872417 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42872417/

**Summary**

The study investigates uniform lithium deposition on polycrystalline copper current collectors by engineering dislocation defects and crystal textures. It demonstrates that high-density geometrically necessary dislocations and enriched <110>/<111> textures enable thermodynamically favorable lithium nucleation and surface diffusion, leading to stable deposition and over 1200 cycles with high Coulombic efficiency. This approach addresses a critical challenge in lithium metal batteries.

**Key findings**

- Densely distributed dislocations provide thermodynamically favorable lithium adsorption sites and heterogeneous nucleation sites
- Enriched <110>/<111> textures promote lithium surface diffusion and lateral growth
- Optimized Cu foils sustain over 1200 cycles with 99.52% average Coulombic efficiency in Li||Cu cells

**Study type**

Experimental with theoretical calculations

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes dislocation-mediated nucleation as a fundamental design principle for achieving stable lithium deposition on practical copper current collectors, advancing the understanding of interfacial mechanisms in lithium metal batteries.

- Biomaterials have evolved from simple nucleases to nanostructured platforms for targeted cfDNA scavenging
- Material architecture influences cfDNA accessibility, stability, tissue distribution, and therapeutic efficacy

**Scientific significance**

This work advances the understanding of cfDNA as a therapeutic target in inflammation by demonstrating how biomaterial design can selectively remove pathogenic cfDNA while maintaining host immune function, providing a foundation for clinical translation.

## Polymeric and metallic occluders for interventional therapy of cardiovascular diseases.

PMID: 42892255 | Journal: Biomaterials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42892255/

**Summary**

This review examines the evolution and design principles of cardiovascular occluders for interventional therapy, covering congenital heart defects and left atrial appendage closure. It compares non-degradable metals (e.g., stainless steel) to nitinol alloys and biodegradable polymers (e.g., polylactic acid), highlighting surface modifications for endothelialization and challenges in degradation kinetics. The study identifies key advancements in materials and design that enhance safety and efficacy for structural heart disease treatment.

**Key findings**

- Nitinol alloys offer hyperelasticity for self-expanding devices in transcatheter closure procedures.
- Surface nanocoatings of bioceramics like titanium nitride promote rapid endothelialization on nitinol implants.
- Biodegradable polymers such as polylactic acid and polydioxanone enable device design strategies to overcome hyperelasticity limitations.

**Scientific significance**

This review provides a comprehensive synthesis of material innovations and design principles that advance the safety and efficacy of interventional therapies for structural heart disease, addressing critical challenges in device performance and biocompatibility.

**Study type**

Experimental and simulation

**Scientific significance**

## Mitochondria-targeted tetraoxanes initiate lipid peroxidation to drive ferroptosis and tumor suppression.

PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42880285/

**Summary**

This study designed mitochondria-targeted tetraoxane compounds (WMK-1/2) to induce ferroptosis by initiating lipid peroxidation without targeting GPX4 or FSP1. The compounds localized to mitochondria, caused local lipid peroxidation, impaired oxidative phosphorylation, and triggered a 'mito-ferroptosis' pathway that requires labile Fe2+ and is suppressed by α-lipoic acid. WMK-1/2 showed tumor suppression efficacy in cancer models and enhanced immune-checkpoint blockade.

**Key findings**

- WMK-1/2 are mitochondria-targeted tetraoxanes that initiate lipid peroxidation to drive ferroptosis
- The 'mito-ferroptosis' pathway skips GPX4 inhibition and requires labile Fe2+ for cell death
- WMK-1/2 suppress tumor growth and augment immune-checkpoint blockade efficacy in cancer models

**Study type**

In vitro and in vivo cancer models

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes a protein-targeting-independent metabolic strategy for ferroptosis induction, offering a novel approach to overcome limitations in current ferroptosis-targeted cancer therapies.

PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42876195/

**Summary**

This study investigates voltage hysteresis in oxygen-redox cathodes, identifying quasi-flat bands as the origin of hysteresis exceeding 1 V during initial cycles. By linking weak lattice distortions and thermal vibrations to amplified band dispersion, the research proposes stabilizing symmetry and engineering orbital hybridization to mitigate hysteresis, enhancing energy efficiency for high-energy lithium batteries.

**Key findings**

- Oxygen 2p states near the Fermi level in quasi-flat bands are sensitive to minor lattice distortions
- Weak perturbations disproportionately amplify band dispersion, causing voltage hysteresis
- Stabilizing symmetry and engineering orbital hybridization reduce hysteresis for practical battery operation

**Study type**

Theoretical modeling and design principles

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes a fundamental mechanism for voltage hysteresis in oxygen-redox cathodes, providing a design framework to enhance energy efficiency in high-capacity lithium batteries.
