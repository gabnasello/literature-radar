## MALT1 is upregulated and its inhibition improves neural injury, behavioral abnormalities, and inflammation in major depressive disorder.

PMID: 43056978 | Journal: Psychiatry research
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43056978/

**Summary**

This study investigated MALT1 expression in major depressive disorder (MDD) patients and MDD mouse models. Using blood samples from MDD patients and healthy controls, and unpredictable chronic mild stress to induce MDD in mice, researchers found elevated MALT1 expression in MDD patients. MALT1 inhibition with high-dose MI-2 reduced neural injury, improved behavioral abnormalities, and decreased inflammation in MDD mice, while lower doses showed minimal effects.

**Key findings**

- MALT1 expression was significantly elevated in MDD patients compared to healthy controls (median 3.41 vs. 1.10, P<0.001).
- High-dose MALT1 inhibition (35 mg/kg MI-2) reduced amygdala neuronal apoptosis and increased Nissl number in MDD mice.
- MALT1 inhibition decreased spleen Th17/Treg ratio, serum IL-17A, and TNF-α levels in MDD mice, improving behavioral outcomes.

**Scientific significance**

This study identifies MALT1 as a potential biomarker and therapeutic target for major depressive disorder, demonstrating that its inhibition alleviates neural injury, behavioral deficits, and inflammatory markers in preclinical models.

## Structure-Encoded Magnetic Anisotropy in Super-Tough Magnetoelastic Aramid-Silk-Magnetite Fibers.

PMID: 42984317 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42984317/

**Summary**

This study investigates how hierarchical structural organization can intrinsically encode magnetic anisotropy in composite fibers. Researchers engineered aramid-silk-magnetite nanofibrils by growing magnetite nanoparticles within an aligned matrix, inspired by biomineralization. The fibers achieved high strength, modulus, and toughness (820 MJ m-3) while exhibiting tunable magnetic anisotropy from axial to radial easy axes. Collective dipolar coupling dominated magnetic anisotropy, enabling strain-mediated reconfiguration of magnetic properties and magnetoelastic actuation.

**Key findings**

- Composite fibers with aramid-silk-magnetite structure achieved exceptional toughness (820 MJ m-3)
- Tunable magnetic anisotropy spanned axial to radial easy-axis configurations via structural relaxation
- Collective dipolar coupling dominated magnetic anisotropy by orders of magnitude over demagnetization effects

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes a structural design principle for programming intrinsic magnetic anisotropy in mechanically robust magnetoelastic materials, enabling strain-controlled magnetic reconfiguration.

## Non-Selective Suppression of Cation and Anion Crosstalk from an Anode Perspective.

PMID: 42984329 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42984329/

**Summary**

This study investigates suppressing electrode crosstalk in metal fluoride and sulfur batteries by designing a Na-rich nanoalloy (NaxSn) anode. The approach combines kinetic regulation via elevated Damköhler numbers and physical shielding through an ultrathin Na2O-rich solid electrolyte interphase. Results show 99.93% Coulombic efficiency over 200 cycles and 91.95% capacity retention after 1000 cycles at 0.5C in FeF2||NaxSn and S/C||NaxSn systems, demonstrating effective suppression of cation and anion crosstalk.

**Key findings**

- NaxSn anodes achieve 99.93% Coulombic efficiency over 200 cycles
- S/C||NaxSn systems retain 91.95% capacity after 1000 cycles at 0.5C
- The Na2O-rich solid electrolyte interphase non-selectively blocks cation and anion crossovers

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes a dual mechanism for suppressing electrode crosstalk in high-energy batteries, enabling long-term cycling stability through spatiotemporally synergistic anode-cathode coupling.

## Coordination Bridges in Confined-Water Domains Enable Partially Desolvated Zn2 + Transport in Aqueous Zinc-Ion Batteries.

PMID: 42985839 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42985839/

**Summary**

This study investigates enhancing zinc-ion battery performance by designing a polymeric protective layer with amide and imidazolium groups. The amide groups stabilize Zn2+ transport via binding sites, while imidazolium groups create confined-water domains that regulate solvation. Water molecules act as transient coordination bridges during Zn2+ migration, improving single-ion conductivity by 6.55-fold and reducing hydrogen evolution by 79.1%. The approach extends cycling lifetimes to 2200 hours in symmetric cells and enables 4000 cycles with >85% capacity retention in full cells.

**Key findings**

- The polymeric layer with amide and imidazolium groups increases Zn2+ single-ion conductivity by 6.55-fold
- Hydrogen evolution rate is reduced by 79.1% due to confined-water domain regulation
- Zn//Zn symmetric cells achieve 2200-hour cycling with 1 mA cm-2 and 1 mAh cm-2

**Scientific significance**

This work demonstrates a dual-functional interfacial strategy that enhances aqueous zinc-ion battery longevity through controlled water-mediated ion transport, advancing the design of stable, high-capacity grid-scale energy storage systems.

PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42985841/

**Summary**

This study investigates phase evolution and crystallization kinetics in DMF-derived kesterite solar cells by introducing an ultrathin CdSe sacrificial layer at the Mo/precursor interface during selenization. The layer rapidly consumes to provide in-situ Se supply, overcoming surface transport delays and enabling early bottom selenization. **Key findings**

- Ultrathin CdSe layer at Mo/precursor interface provides in-situ Se supply during selenization

- Redistributed Cd2+ reduces defect-assisted recombination and enhances carrier transport

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes a method to coordinate phase evolution and crystallization kinetics in kesterite absorbers by enabling in-situ Se release at the buried interface, addressing a fundamental transport limitation in multinary chalcogenide solar cells.
