## Quantitative measurement of viscosity in two-dimensional electron fluids.

PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43019403/

**Summary**

This study investigates electron viscosity in two-dimensional electron fluids using magnetoresistance in Corbino-shaped graphene devices. The approach disentangles ohmic and viscous transport contributions, revealing that the effective electron-electron scattering rate scales linearly with temperature in monolayer and bilayer graphene. This finding contradicts conventional Fermi liquid predictions and aligns with a tomographic fluid regime, suggesting non-Fermi liquid behavior in 2D electron systems.

**Key findings**

- Magnetoresistance in Corbusing graphene devices simultaneously measures ohmic and viscous transport contributions
- Effective electron-electron scattering rate scales linearly with temperature in monolayer and bilayer graphene
- The linear temperature dependence aligns with a tomographic fluid regime, not conventional Fermi liquid behavior

**Scientific significance**

The findings demonstrate non-Fermi liquid behavior in two-dimensional electron systems, providing quantitative evidence for a tomographic fluid regime that challenges conventional transport models.
