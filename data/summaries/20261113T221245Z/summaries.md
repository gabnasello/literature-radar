## Hypoxia-Tolerant Supramolecular Nanomicelles for Pyroptosis-Enhanced NIR-II Phototheranostics.

PMID: 42876433 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42876433/

**Summary**

This study engineered hypoxia-tolerant supramolecular nanomicelles (TBBP NPs) to enhance NIR-II phototheranostics. The approach combined molecular conformation engineering with supramolecular assembly, enabling efficient photothermal conversion, Type I photodynamic therapy, and immunogenic pyroptosis under hypoxia. In 4T1 tumors, TBBP NPs achieved 92% primary tumor inhibition and 47.7% distant tumor inhibition via synergistic imaging, phototherapy, and immune activation.

**Key findings**

- TBBP NPs exhibit 42.17% photothermal conversion efficiency and broadband NIR-II emission (1000-1600 nm)
- TBBP NPs induce caspase-3/GSDME-mediated immunogenic pyroptosis, promoting dendritic cell maturation and CD8+ T cell infiltration
- In 4T1 tumors, TBBP NPs achieved 92.0% primary tumor inhibition and 47.7% distant tumor inhibition through synergistic Type I PDT and photothermal therapy

**Study type**

In vivo tumor model

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes a molecular conformation engineering strategy for hypoxia-tolerant phototheranostic nanocarriers that synergistically integrate imaging, phototherapy, and immunotherapy, providing a generalizable design principle for multifunctional cancer theranostics.

## Programming hemostasis and healing via charge-density modulation in a thermo-responsive hydrogel.

PMID: 42906522 | Journal: Biomaterials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42906522/

**Summary**

This study investigates programming hemostasis and healing through charge-density modulation in thermo-responsive hydrogels. By tuning ε-polylysine cationic density to create EPL-C2, the researchers developed a fibrinogen-selective hydrogel (FN-C2) that enhances hemostasis without excessive coagulation activation. FN-C2 also enabled sustained insulin delivery, accelerating wound healing via improved granulation tissue and collagen deposition.

**Key findings**

- EPL-C2 selectively interacts with fibrinogen over plasma proteins
- FN-C2 hydrogel enhances hemostasis without broad coagulation cascade activation
- FN-C2 with insulin delivery accelerates skin and intestinal wound healing

**Study type**

In vitro and in vivo studies

**Scientific significance**

This work demonstrates a universal design strategy for programmable hemostatic interfaces that inherently support integrated therapeutic delivery, advancing wound dressing technology through charge-density optimization.

## Perforin triggers iron-induced mitochondrial damage to drive ferroptosis and anti-tumor immunity.

PMID: 42914978 | Journal: Cell
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42914978/

**Summary**

This study investigates perforin's role in anti-tumor immunity by revealing a Gzm-independent mechanism where perforin induces ferroptosis in tumor cells. Perforin forms pores that increase iron uptake via transferrin receptor endocytosis, leading to mitochondrial iron accumulation, dysfunction, and ferroptosis. Dietary iron supplementation reduced tumor growth in mice, and higher perforin expression correlated with better patient survival and immune checkpoint response.

**Key findings**

- Perforin directly triggers ferroptosis in tumor cells without granzymes
- Perforin increases cellular iron uptake by promoting transferrin receptor endocytosis
- Higher perforin expression correlates with improved patient survival and immune checkpoint response

**Study type**

Murine tumor models and human patient data

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes perforin as a direct executor of ferroptosis in cancer cell death, providing a novel mechanism for perforin's role in anti-tumor immunity beyond granzyme-dependent pathways.

## Rare-Earth-Cluster-Mediated Long-Range Electronic Modulation Activates Zn Atomic Sites for Oxygen Reduction Catalysis.

PMID: 42877950 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42877950/

**Summary**

This study investigates using rare-earth oxide clusters to modulate electronic structures of zinc single-atom sites for enhanced oxygen reduction reaction (ORR) catalysis. By engineering LaOx clusters proximal to Zn sites on porous carbon, the catalyst achieves a high half-wave potential of 0.90 V and stable performance in alkaline media. It demonstrates superior ORR activity in non-precious-metal electrocatalysts with a zinc-air battery power density of 177.3 mW cm-2.

**Key findings**

- LaOx clusters remotely modulate Zn site electronic structures to enhance ORR activity
- Catalyst achieves 0.90 V half-wave potential and 177.3 mW cm-2 power density in zinc-air batteries
- Theoretical calculations confirm improved electron transfer efficiency via strengthened Zn site interactions

**Study type**

Electrocatalysis study with single-atom catalysts

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes a strategy for long-range electronic modulation of single-atom sites using rare-earth clusters, advancing understanding of how to engineer electrocatalytic activity through hierarchical electronic interactions.

**Scientific significance**

This work demonstrates a modular biomaterial strategy for systemically administered aAPCs that coordinate molecular immune signaling with organ-level biodistribution, enabling endogenous antigen-specific T-cell priming without genetic modification.

## In vivo genome-editing therapy for Duchenne muscular dystrophy: Preclinical studies and first-in-human trial.

PMID: 42933389 | Journal: Cell
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42933389/

**Summary**

This study evaluates in vivo genome editing using AAV9 vectors for Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD) therapy in preclinical models and early human trials. It demonstrated targeted dystrophin restoration in mice and primates with no dose-limiting toxicity, but a fatal adverse event occurred in one participant at the highest dose due to complement activation and cytokine release. The findings highlight immunologic risks and the narrow therapeutic window for AAV-CRISPR therapies.

**Key findings**

- Targeted genome editing restored partial dystrophin in humanized DMD mice
- Efficient on-target editing with no dose-limiting toxicity in non-human primates
- One participant experienced fatal adverse event at high dose due to complement activation and cytokine response

**Study type**

Open-label, dose-escalation clinical trial with preclinical models

**Scientific significance**

The study provides initial human proof-of-concept for muscle genome editing while emphasizing the critical immunologic risks and narrow therapeutic window of systemic AAV-CRISPR therapies, necessitating safer dosing strategies.

## Cyclic Nonequilibrium Processing of Halide Solid Electrolytes for Low-Pressure Operation of all-Solid-State Batteries.

PMID: 42878139 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42878139/

**Summary**

This study investigates enhancing ionic conductivity and deformability in inorganic solid electrolytes for all-solid-state batteries. By applying cyclic nonequilibrium thermal processing to Li3InCl6, the researchers optimized particle size, induced local amorphization, and created Li vacancies. This approach increased ionic conductivity from 1.3 to 6.8 mS/cm and reduced Young's modulus from 42.3 to 17.6 GPa, enabling stable battery operation at 2 MPa.

**Key findings**

- Cyclic nonequilibrium thermal processing of Li3InCl6 improved ionic conductivity from 1.3 to 6.8 mS/cm
- Reduction in Young's modulus from 42.3 to 17.6 GPa enhanced compressibility
- Optimized solid electrolyte enabled stable cycling at low pressure (2 MPa) with high areal capacity (7.7 mAh/cm²)

**Study type**

Cyclic nonequilibrium thermal processing

**Scientific significance**

This work demonstrates a structural engineering strategy to overcome mechanical and ionic transport barriers in solid electrolytes, providing a pathway to practical low-pressure all-solid-state batteries.

## Long-range actin-driven endosymbiont mobility in an animal-algal symbiosis.

PMID: 42950816 | Journal: Cell
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42950816/

**Summary**

This study investigates whether symbionts in animal-microbe symbioses remain immobile after losing motile appendages. Using long-term tracking, fluorescence, and electron microscopy, researchers observed dinoflagellate symbionts moving long-range within acoel worm hosts via host actin machinery. The symbionts traveled at ~1 μm/s within ultra-thin host parenchymal cells, with mobility regulated by diel rhythms and regeneration. The findings reveal a host-driven mechanism for maintaining symbiont distribution in dynamic homeostasis.

**Key findings**

- Dinoflagellate symbionts move long-range within aco:el worm hosts at ~1 μm/s
- Mobility is driven by host actin machinery despite confinement in ultra-thin parenchymal cells
- Symbiont distribution is spatiotemporally regulated by diel rhythms and regeneration

**Study type**

Long-term tracking, fluorescence, and electron microscopy

**Scientific significance**

This work demonstrates a host-cytoskeleton-driven mechanism for active symbiont transport, expanding understanding of how holobionts maintain dynamic homeostasis through host-directed mobility.

## From Dark Photocatalysis to Photobattery-Type Operation: A Re-Functionalized Ti-MOF for Solar Energy Storage.

PMID: 42885334 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42885334/

**Summary**

This study investigates Re-functionalized Ti-MOFs for solar energy storage, demonstrating their ability to harvest, store, and release photogenerated charge under visible light. The researchers synthesized COK47-bpy and ReCl47-bpy, showing Re incorporation enhances charge retention, enables visible-light response, and allows on-demand hydrogen evolution with >90% efficiency. **Key findings**

- Re-functionalized Ti-MOFs (ReCl47-bpy) achieve 19.5 C g-1 electron storage capacity in colloidal suspension under solar irradiation
- The material retains 70% of stored charge after 2.5 months and enables >90% electron-to-hydrogen evolution efficiency
- FTO-supported ReCl47-bpy photoelectrodes deliver up to 7.0 mAh g-1 capacity under 1 Sun irradiation

**Study type**

Synthetic photochargeable materials

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes Re-functionalized Ti-MOFs as versatile platforms for solar-driven charge storage and dark photocatalysis, advancing the development of MOF-based energy devices for continuous solar energy utilization.

## Somatic DNA-repeat expansions initiate distinct yet analogous changes in Huntington's disease cortical and striatal neurons.

PMID: 42950817 | Journal: Cell
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42950817/

**Summary**

This study investigates whether somatic CAG-repeat expansions in Huntington's disease neurons follow similar pathological pathways in cortical and striatal regions. Using molecular and pathological analyses, it found that both neuron types exhibit analogous sequence of pathological phases starting at ∼150 CAGs, with distinct gene expression changes and 20-fold variation in expansion magnitude among cortical pyramidal neuron subtypes. Both regions develop nuclear inclusions of aggregated HTT protein beyond 150 CAGs.

**Key findings**

- Cortical pyramidal neurons undergo somatic CAG-repeat expansions starting at ∼150 CAGs, analogous to striatal neurons
- Expansion magnitude varies up to 20-fold among cortical pyramidal neuron subtypes
- Both cortical and striatal neurons form nuclear inclusions of aggregated HTT protein beyond 150 CAGs

**Study type**

Molecular and pathological analysis

**Scientific significance**

The findings demonstrate that somatic CAG-repeat expansions initiate comparable pathological sequences in distinct brain regions of Huntington's disease, revealing shared mechanisms of early neuronal degeneration despite regional differences in gene expression.

## "Water-in-Water" Biphasic Electrolyte for Aqueous Zinc-ion Batteries.

PMID: 42885335 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42885335/

**Summary**

This study addresses unstable Zn metal interfaces in aqueous zinc-ion batteries by engineering a 'water-in-water' biphasic electrolyte with dextran droplets in a PEG-rich phase. The architecture buffers local Zn2+ accumulation, enabling stable cycling for 1100 hours at high current density and Ah-level pouch cells. The findings highlight interfacial electrostatics as the governing mechanism for Zn2+ transport.

**Key findings**

- A 'water-in-water' biphasic electrolyte with dextran droplets in PEG-rich phase buffers local Zn2+ accumulation
- Zn||Zn cells cycle stably for 1100 h at 10 mA cm-2 and 10 mAh cm-2
- Ah-level Zn||NaV3O8 pouch cells sustain long-term operation

**Study type**

Electrolyte engineering

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes interfacial electrostatics as the primary mechanism for regulating Zn2+ transport in aqueous zinc-ion batteries, overcoming traditional limitations of solvent chemistry.

## Cation-Framework-Preserving Oxygen-Vacancy Engineering for Broadband Infrared Emission in High-Entropy Spinel Oxides.

PMID: 42885381 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42885381/

**Summary**

This study investigates oxygen vacancy engineering in high-entropy spinel oxides to achieve broadband infrared emission while maintaining structural stability. By vacuum annealing (MnCrFeNiCu)3O4 without altering cation composition, researchers enhanced emissivity from 0.668-0.726 to 0.932-0.902 across 0.78-16 µm. The approach preserves the spinel framework and demonstrates that oxygen vacancies can improve emission without compromising thermal stability.

**Key findings**

- Vacuum annealing of (MnCrFeNiCu)3O4 increased infrared emissivity to 0.932-0.902 across 0.78-16 µm
- Oxygen vacancies created vacancy-derived states and redistributed charge without degrading the spinel framework
- The high-entropy spinel maintained structural integrity after 1000°C annealing for 5 hours

**Study type**

Vacuum annealing of high-entropy spinel oxide

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes a method to engineer oxygen vacancies in multication spinels for broadband infrared emission while retaining high-temperature structural stability, providing a pathway to enhance thermal emission in advanced ceramics.

## Photocatalytic Tandem Reaction for Urea Synthesis From Atmospheric CO2 and N2.

PMID: 42885386 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42885386/

**Summary**

This study investigates a photocatalytic tandem system for direct urea synthesis from atmospheric CO2 and N2, overcoming the energy-intensive Bosch-Meiser process. The IL@MOF captures CO2 and reduces it to CO, while the HTP-Cu3-imi-COF couples CO with N2 into urea. The system achieves a urea production rate of 259.87 µg·gcat⁻¹·h⁻¹ under air, representing the first photocatalytic urea synthesis from atmospheric gases.

**Key findings**

- IL@MOF captures atmospheric CO2 and photoreduces it to CO
- HTP-Cu3-imi-COF couples CO with ambient N2 into urea
- The tandem system achieves 259.87 µg·gcat⁻¹·h⁻¹ urea production rate under air

**Study type**

Photocatalytic tandem platform

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes the first photocatalytic urea synthesis from atmospheric CO2 and N2, demonstrating a modular cascade catalyst design that integrates capture and conversion for sustainable chemical production.
