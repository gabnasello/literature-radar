## Persistent neuronal stress signalling underlies Angelman syndrome phenotypes.

PMID: 42907508 | Journal: Nature
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42907508/

**Summary**

This study investigates the mechanisms of Angelman syndrome, caused by UBE3A loss. Using gain- and loss-of-function mutations and proteomics, it identifies asparagine synthetase (ASNS) as a UBE3A substrate. ASNS overabundance in Ube3a-null mice and human neurons activates stress signaling, reduces neuronal protein synthesis, and rescues microcephaly and behavioral deficits when corrected genetically. The findings suggest persistent neuronal stress signaling underlies Angelman syndrome phenotypes.

**Key findings**

- ASNS is a UBE3A substrate with increased abundance in Ube3a-null mice and human neurons
- ASNS overabundance activates stress signaling and reduces neuronal protein synthesis
- Genetic correction of ASNS overabundance rescues microcephaly and behavioral deficits

**Scientific significance**

The study identifies a specific stress signaling pathway activated by UBE3A loss that contributes to Angelman syndrome pathophysiology, providing a potential therapeutic target.

## A single archaeon couples long-chain fatty acid oxidation to methanogenesis.

PMID: 42907509 | Journal: Nature
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42907509/

**Summary**

This study isolates a thermophilic archaeon, Methanoglobus dolicholipophagus AA-75, capable of lipotrophic methanogenesis. It directly oxidizes long-chain fatty acids (C7:0–C18:0) and unsaturated fatty acids to methane, integrating β-oxidation with methanogenesis pathways. The archaeon produces 13C-enriched methane, challenging the syntrophic cooperation model and indicating methanogens can independently degrade complex organic substrates in deep subsurface environments.

**Key findings**

- The archaeon oxidizes saturated fatty acids from C7:0 to C18:0 and unsaturated fatty acids like C16:1, C18:1, and C18:2 to produce methane.
- Lipotrophic methanogenesis integrates β-oxidation with the Wood-Ljungdahl pathway and methyl-tetrahydromethanopterin:coenzyme M methyltransferase complexes.
- Methane from stearate shows moderate 13C-enrichment (δ13C-CH4 = -40.0‰ to -36.3‰), distinct from typical methanogenic pathways.

**Scientific significance**

This work demonstrates methanogens can independently degrade complex organic matter, challenging the classical syntrophic paradigm and refining models of carbon cycling in deep subsurface biospheres.

Researchers identified defect-induced localized states in Pd5AlI2, a van der Waals metal, which remain stable within the metallic continuum. - Atomic defects shift localized states into the metallic continuum, forming symmetry-protected bound states

**Scientific significance**

## Amazon carbon emissions driven by human actions amplified by droughts.

PMID: 42907510 | Journal: Nature
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42907510/

**Summary**

This study analyzed 15 years of Amazonian carbon fluxes using atmospheric CO2 measurements to assess how human activities and droughts impact carbon emissions. It found that deforestation, biomass burning, and climate stressors like El Niño amplify carbon emissions, with forest uptake increasingly failing to offset emissions. The carbon balance shows a net emission of 0.32 ± 0.08 PgC yr-1, driven primarily by fires.

**Key findings**

- Amazon carbon emissions increased to 0.49 ± 0.07 PgC yr-1 during high climate stress years (e.g., El Niño) with significant fire activity
- Human-driven deforestation and degradation contributed 0.43 ± 0.13 PgC yr-1 during low climate stress periods
- Forest carbon uptake became progressively unable to offset emissions, resulting in a net carbon balance of 0.32 ± 0.08 PgC yr-1

**Scientific significance**

The research demonstrates that human-induced deforestation and climate stressors synergistically amplify Amazon carbon emissions, revealing critical thresholds for forest carbon sink collapse.

## Grain-Boundary-Facilitated Palladium Hydride Formation Enables Humidity-Tolerant Catalytic Hydrogen Sensing.

PMID: 42920105 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42920105/

**Summary**

This study investigates a palladium nanostructure with grain boundaries (PdGB) for humidity-tolerant hydrogen sensing. The researchers developed a sensor operating without external heating that functions across 22°C-80°C and 0%-90% relative humidity. It detects hydrogen at 100 ppm even under high humidity conditions, meeting ISO 2.6142 standards. Mechanistic studies show grain boundaries accelerate palladium hydride formation to weaken water adsorption, enabling reliable operation in humid environments.

**Key findings**

- PdGB sensors operate without external heating across 22°C-80°C and 0%-90% relative humidity
- Achieves 100 ppm hydrogen detection limit at 80°C and 90% humidity (261 g m-3 absolute humidity)
- Grain boundaries facilitate palladium hydride formation to suppress water interference in hydrogen sensing

**Scientific significance**

This work identifies grain boundaries as a critical design feature for humidity-tolerant catalytic hydrogen sensors, providing a general strategy to overcome water interference in gas sensing applications.

## Cesium Tin Iodide Perovskites Engineer the Sn4+/Sn2+ Redox Reaction in Aqueous Zinc-Ion Batteries.

PMID: 42920376 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42920376/

**Summary**

This study investigates engineering the Sn4+/Sn2+ redox reaction in aqueous zinc-ion batteries using cesium tin iodide perovskites. The approach involves in situ growth of single and double perovskite layers on the cathode, enabling the first demonstration of the Sn4+/Sn2+ redox couple. Results show increased potential to 0.39 V vs. SHE, dual discharge plateaus, high areal capacity, and exceptional cycling stability in full batteries.

**Key findings**

- First demonstration of Sn4+/Sn2+ redox reaction in aqueous zinc-ion batteries
- Increased redox potential to 0.39 V vs. SHE via enhanced tin-halide coordination
- High areal capacity (0.80 mAh cm-2) and long cycling stability (7000 cycles at 16 mA cm-2)

**Scientific significance**

This work enables high-potential redox reactions in aqueous zinc-ion batteries through perovskite interlayers, advancing energy storage technology with enhanced capacity and stability.
