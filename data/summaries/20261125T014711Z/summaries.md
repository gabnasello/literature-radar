## Cross-Scale Decoupling of Mechanics and Kinematics in Chiral Tensegrity Metamaterials.

PMID: 42989573 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42989573/

**Summary**

This study investigates cross-scale decoupling strategies in chiral tensegrity metamaterials to resolve trade-offs between deformation capacity, stiffness, and durability. The researchers designed a chiral three-rod tensegr. cell that mechanically decouples macroscopic shape changes from local strain, achieving 97° torsion with minimal force degradation over 10,000 cycles. Kinematic decoupling at the assembly scale enables rotationally constrained interfaces while maintaining pure translation. The framework demonstrates potential for integrating metamaterial physics with robotic systems in constrained environments.

**Key findings**

- Mechanical decoupling at unit-cell scale achieves 97° torsion with only 4.87% peak-force degradation after 10,000 cycles
- Kinematic decoupling localizes torsional deformation as a boundary-decoupled internal mode
- Post-fabrication programmability of stiffness and strain-normalized twist ratio via geometric interlocking

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes a cross-scale decoupling framework that resolves fundamental trade-offs in chiral metamaterials, enabling robust torsional deformation and interface compatibility for applications in constrained robotic systems.

## CCR8⁺ regulatory T cell depletion overcomes immune checkpoint blockade resistance in BRCA2-deficient prostate cancer.

PMID: 43060203 | Journal: bioRxiv : the preprint server for biology
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43060203/

**Summary**

This study investigated how genomic instability in BRCA2-mutated prostate cancer affects tumor immunity. Using an isogenic model, researchers found BRCA2 loss activated the cGAS/STING pathway, causing immunosuppression via CCR8⁺ regulatory T cells and Ccl8-expressing macrophages. Anti-PD-1 therapy exacerbated immunosone suppression, but CCR8⁺ Treg depletion reversed resistance, with human data confirming elevated CCR8 in BRCA2-mutated tumors, suggesting CCR8⁺ Tregs as a target for immunotherapy.

**Key findings**

- BRCA2 deficiency activates cGAS/STING pathway, inducing immunosuppression
- CCR8⁺ regulatory T cells and Ccl8-expressing macrophages drive immunosuppression
- Depleting CCR8⁺ regulatory T cells overcomes anti-PD-1 resistance in BRCA2-deficient tumors

**Scientific significance**

This work identifies CCR8⁺ regulatory T cells as a key immunosuppressive mechanism in BRCA2-deficient prostate cancer, providing a target to overcome immunotherapy resistance in this aggressive subtype.

## Threshold-Voltage Ion Sieving in Ultrathin Polymeric Membranes for Energy-Efficient Lithium Extraction.

PMID: 42989582 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42989582/

**Summary**

This study investigates multi-ion discrimination in ultrathin polymeric membranes using aligned porphyrin pores. The researchers fabricated a membrane (~8.56 nm thick) via interfacial superspreading, achieving simultaneous separation of six ion species under microvolt-scale voltages. The membrane shows anomalous Li+ selectivity with a threshold voltage of ~400 mV, enabling >98% lithium recovery from brines at ultra-low energy consumption (1.1 × 1.0-4 Wh g-1).

**Key findings**

- Ultrathin polymeric membranes with angstrom-scale porphyrin pores achieve simultaneous multi-ion discrimination
- The membrane exhibits anomalous Li+ selectivity with a threshold voltage of ~400 mV
- Two-stage devices recover >98% lithium from brines at 1.1 × 10-4 Wh g-1 energy consumption

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes a design principle for energy-efficient ion separation by leveraging threshold-voltage sieving in ultrathin membranes, enabling high selectivity for ions of similar charge and size without conventional energy penalties.

## Clonally Convergent Tph/Tfh-like CD4 + T Cells are Shared Across Recovery and Recurrence in Experimental Periodontitis.

PMID: 43060255 | Journal: bioRxiv : the preprint server for biology
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43060255/

**Summary**

This study investigated whether clinical recovery in experimental periodontitis resets the local T cell repertoire. Using a mouse model with induced regulatory breakdown, researchers mapped gingival T cell dynamics across disease phases. They identified a clonally convergent CD4⁺ T cell population with Tfh/Tph-like transcriptional identity that persisted from recovery to recurrence. Single-cell analyses in human lesions confirmed a comparable population, suggesting a reservoir of antigen-experienced cells contributes to disease relapse.

**Key findings**

- A clonally convergent CD4⁺ T cell population with Tfh/Tph-like transcriptional identity persisted from recovery to recurrence in mice
- This population was detected in human periodontitis lesions via single-cell transcriptomics
- Clinical recovery did not reset the local T cell repertoire, indicating a reservoir of tissue-adapted antigen-experienced cells

**Scientific significance**

The findings establish a persistent reservoir of antigen-experienced CD4⁺ T cells that can drive periodontitis recurrence, revealing a mechanism for disease relapse beyond conventional immune memory.

## Ionization-State-Directed Sulfonate-Rich Buried Interfaces Enable Durable High-Efficiency Perovskite Solar Cells and Modules.

PMID: 42989701 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42989701/

**Summary**

This study investigates ionization-state-directed sulfonate engineering to enhance stability in inverted perovskite solar cells. Using sodium 2,2'-disulfanediyldiethanesulfonate (DDANA), the researchers created sulfonate-rich interfaces that form robust multisite binding with perovskite, optimize energy alignment, and scavenge corrosive iodine. The approach achieved 27.38% certified efficiency in cells, 25.88% in flexible devices, and 23.33% in modules, with >98% retention after 1000 hours of operation.

**Key findings**

- DDANA forms multisite binding with perovskite lattice via sulfonate groups
- Sulfonate-enriched interfaces optimize energy level alignment and hole transport
- DDANA-modified devices retain >98% efficiency after 1.000 hours of maximum power point tracking

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes sulfonate ionization-state engineering as a versatile strategy for developing chemically robust buried interfaces in perovskite solar cells, advancing stability and scalability without compromising efficiency.

## Cathode-Driven Aluminothermic Fronts Amplify Thermal Runaway in Lithium-Ion Batteries.

PMID: 42989709 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42989709/

**Summary**

This study investigates thermal runaway mechanisms in lithium-ion batteries by identifying cathode-driven aluminothermic fronts as a heat-amplification pathway. Using semi-1D composite bars in argon, researchers isolated interfacial redox chemistry between nickel-rich layered oxide cathodes and aluminum. The aluminothermic fronts propagated at velocities comparable to thermal-runaway fronts, with propagation rates dependent on aluminum loading, cathode oxygen-donor capacity, and oxygen availability. Energy-audit calculations suggest this reaction releases heat equivalent to half the stored electrical energy, indicating a significant internal heat-amplification mechanism relevant to battery safety.

**Key findings**

- Aluminothermic fronts propagate at velocities matching thermal-runaway fronts in argon
- Propagation rates depend on aluminum loading, cathode oxygen-donor capacity, and oxygen availability
- The reaction releases heat equivalent to approximately half the stored electrical energy

**Scientific significance**

This work identifies cathode-aluminum redox chemistry as a critical internal heat-amusing mechanism that accelerates thermal runaway in lithium-ion batteries, providing new insights for battery safety design.

## Extracellular vesicles in osteoporosis and osteoimmunity: a systematic evidence map.

PMID: 43060979 | Journal: Frontiers in physiology
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43060979/

**Summary**

This systematic evidence map analyzed 222 primary studies on osteoporosis extracellular vesicles to identify research concentrations across osteoimmune mechanisms, mechanistic depth, and human evidence. It found 90.5% used animal models, 51.8% employed advanced mechanistic perturbation, and only 16.2% included human data. The remodeling interface was the most studied module, while few studies connected donor states to osteoimmune mediators and human outcomes.

**Key findings**

- 90.5% of studies used animal models, with only 16.2% linking to human data
- 51.8% of studies applied advanced mechanistic perturbation, but 84 studies lacked human evidence
- The remodeling interface module comprised the largest group (n=120) of studies

**Scientific significance**

This evidence map clarifies gaps in translating osteoporosis extracellular vesicle research from mechanistic models to human applications by identifying underutilized study designs and untested pathways connecting donor states to skeletal outcomes.

Researchers created a phase-controlled spoof surface acoustic wave platform on a perforated acoustic metasusing two twisted skyrmion lattices superposed to form controllable acoustic skyrmion bags.
