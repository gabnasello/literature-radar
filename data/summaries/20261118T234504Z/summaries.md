## Switchable Chlorine Atom Transfer Radical Reactions.

PMID: 42951334 | Journal: Nature
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42951334/

**Summary**

This study investigates a dual-copper catalytic system enabling switchable chlorine atom transfer radical reactions. It uses unactivated alk. chlorides to perform atom transfer radical addition (ATRA) and atom transfer radical polymerization (ATRP) by selectively activating C-Cl bonds and switching reaction pathways based on catalyst presence. The approach allows synthesis of diverse small molecules and α-end-functionalized polyacrylates while modifying PVC under mild conditions.

**Key findings**

- A dinuclear copper complex activates unactivated alkyl chlorides for C-Cl bond cleavage
- Mononuclear copper catalyst enables controllable C-Cl bond reconstruction
- Switchable pathway between ATRA and ATRP reactions via catalyst presence

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes a versatile platform for chlorine atom transfer radical reactions using unactivated organochlorides, advancing synthetic and polymer chemistry by enabling mild modification of PVC and diverse functionalized products.

## Emotion regulation capacity lacks a shared neural signature.

PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42951968/

**Summary**

This study investigated whether emotion regulation capacity shares a common neural signature using cognitive reappraisal as a measure. Analyzing 40 fMRI datasets from 2,175 participants, it found that three indicators of reappraisal capacity—trait questionnaires, task-based affective ratings, and amygdala down-regulation—lack convergence on a single neural core. None showed expected links with prefrontal regulatory circuits, challenging the assumption that such brain activity reflects emotion regulation ability.

**Key findings**

- Three indicators of reappraisal capacity do not converge on a shared neural signature
- Task-based affective ratings were linked to amygdala and sensorimotor cortices rather than regulatory regions

**Scientific significance**

The findings challenge the assumption that prefrontal regulatory circuit activation reliably indicates emotion regulation capacity, suggesting neural biomarkers for this trait must account for non-specific neural engagement during emotional processing.
