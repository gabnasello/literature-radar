Using human neural progenitor cells, researchers identified transcriptomic convergence among 18 ASD genes, revealing shared downregulation of ASD genes. De novo gene regulatory network reconstruction highlighted CHD8 and REST as central regulators, while X-linked ZFX expression in females was proposed to buffer ASD gene mutations, explaining FPE.

**Key findings**

- Individual perturbations of 1:8 ASD genes converge on shared downregulation of ASD genes in human neural progenitor cells
- Central transcriptional regulators CHD8 and REST drive transcriptomic convergence among ASD genes
- X-linked ZFX expression in females buffers ASD gene mutations, contributing to the female protective effect

**Scientific significance**

## Targeting hormone conversion through dietary fiber improves polyendocrine metabolic ovarian syndrome.

PMID: 42986964 | Journal: Science
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42986964/

**Summary**

This study investigated how dietary fiber impacts androgen levels in polyendocrine metabolic ovarian syndrome (PMOS). Using a clinical trial with inulin, resistant starch, and β-glucan, researchers observed reduced plasma androgens. Mechanistically, dietary fiber increased Bacteroides xylanisolvens, which converts androstenedione to coproandrosterone—a nonandrogenic metabolite—further processed into dihydro-coproandroone. The pathway was confirmed in mice, suggesting dietary fiber modulates androgen homeostasis via gut microbiota to alleviate PMOS.

**Key findings**

- Dietary fiber combinations reduced plasma androgen concentrations in PMOS patients
- Bacteroides xylanisolvens increased and converted androstenedione to coproandrosterone
- The coproandrosterone pathway was validated in mouse models

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes a gut microbiota-mediated mechanism by which dietary fiber regulates androgen metabolism, providing a foundation for targeted interventions in PMOS.
