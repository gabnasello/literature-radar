## Cellular scaling laws in the mammalian brain.

PMID: 42880229 | Journal: Cell
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42880229/

**Summary**

This study investigates why deep subcortical brain regions exhibit greater neuronal diversity than cortical areas despite having fewer neurons. Using whole-brain cell atlases, it proposes a dual subsystem model where the 'learning subsystem' scales through repetitive module replication, while the 'steering subsystem' diversifies specialized cell types for innate behaviors. The findings suggest evolutionary mechanisms underlying brain architecture and potential implications for AI model design.

**Key findings**

- Deep subcusing regions like the hypothalamus and brainstem show higher neuronal diversity than cortical areas
- The learning subsystem (cortex, striatum, cerebellum) scales via repetitive module replication
- The steering subsystem (hypothalamus, pallidum, brainstem) diversifies specialized cell types for innate drives

**Scientific significance**

This framework provides a unified evolutionary explanation for brain architecture divergence between learning and steering subsystems, advancing understanding of how neural diversity supports innate behaviors and potential AI applications.
