## Layered Spin-Crossover Metal-Organic Frameworks for Light-Induced Control of Two-Dimensional Quantum Materials.

PMID: 42876357 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42876357/

**Summary**

This study investigates layered spin-crossover metal-organic frameworks (MOFs) for light-induced control of two-dimensional quantum materials. The researchers integrated a Hofmann-type SCO MOF into van der Waals heterostructures with few-layer graphene, CrSBr, and NbSe2. Light-induced spin transitions modulated electronic transport in graphene, induced magnetic hysteresis in CrSBr spin-valves, and altered superconducting properties in NbSe2. These findings demonstrate strain-mediated control of quantum material functionalities via stimuli-responsive MOFs.

**Key findings**

- Light-induced spin transitions selectively switch graphene conductivity
- MOF integration triggers magnetic hysteresis in CrSBr bilayers
- Light modulates superconducting critical current and transition temperature in NbSe2

**Study type**

Electrical nanodevices with van der Waals heterostructures

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes layered spin-crossover MOFs as molecular switches for strain-mediated control of electronic, magnetic, and superconducting properties in two-dimensional quantum materials.

PMID: 42892255 | Journal: Biomaterials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42892255/

The study identifies key challenges in matching degradation rates with tissue healing and future directions for pediatric and elderly applications.

**Scientific significance**

This review advances understanding of occluder material evolution and design principles, providing a foundation for safer, more effective interventional therapies in structural heart disease.

## Metazoan lagging-strand DNA synthesis is distributive.

PMID: 42913629 | Journal: Cell
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42913629/

**Summary**

This study investigates how metazoan lagging-strand DNA synthesis coordinates with leading-strand replication. Using single-molecule imaging in Xenopus extracts, researchers visualized polymerases Pol α and Pol δ at replication forks. They discovered neither polymerase remains stably attached to the replisome; instead, lagging-strand synthesis occurs distributively via rapid recruitment of new polymerase molecules per Okazaki fragment. This indicates leading- and lagging-strand synthesis operate independently without intrinsic coupling.

**Key findings**

- Lagging-strand synthesis in metazoans occurs distributively, requiring rapid recruitment of new Pol α and Pol δ for each Okazaki fragment
- Neither Pol α nor Pol δ is stably tethered to the replisome during replication
- Leading- and lagging-strand synthesis are not intrinsically coupled in metazoans

**Study type**

Single-molecule imaging in Xenopus nuclear extracts

**Scientific significance**

This work reveals that metazoans resolve the topological challenge of anti-parallel DNA replication by enabling distributive lagging-strand synthesis without active coordination between leading and lagging strands.

**Key findings**

**Study type**

Transient absorption spectroscopy

**Scientific significance**

The findings establish a framework for interpreting transient absorption features in Y6 and reveal that polaron formation arises from Onsager-like autoionization of delocalized excitons in interacting domains, providing mechanistic insight into charge generation in nonfullerene acceptor solar cells.
