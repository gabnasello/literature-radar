## An integrative single-cell epigenomic atlas annotates the regulatory genome of the adult mouse brain.

PMID: 43020033 | Journal: Cell
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43020033/

**Summary**

This study integrated single-cell epigenomic data from 2.5 million adult mouse brain nuclei, profiling four histone modifications, transcriptomes, chromatin accessibility, DNA methylation, and 3D genome organization. It constructed a comprehensive atlas identifying 100+ cell subclasses and assigned chromatin states across 81% of the genome, revealing that combinatorial histone marks better predict functional enhancers than accessibility alone, while specific repressive marks define cell-type-specific silencing mechanisms.

**Key findings**

- The atlas identifies 100+ distinct brain cell subclasses using integrated epigenomic data
- Combinatorial histone modifications more accurately predict functional enhancers than chromatin accessibility alone
- H3K27me3 and H3K9me3 mark distinct repressive chromatin states for cell-type-specific gene silencing

**Scientific significance**

This multimodal epigenomic atlas provides a foundational resource for understanding brain cell-type-specific regulatory mechanisms and interpreting non-coding genetic variants associated with neurological disorders.

- FAST circuits activate pyroptosis in cancer cells, causing tumor regression in mouse models without normal tissue toxicity

**Scientific significance**

## Adaptive Supramolecular Interactions Govern Redox-Active Molecules in Aqueous Redox Flow Batteries.

PMID: 42935117 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42935117/

**Summary**

This study investigates how weak noncovalent interactions influence redox-active molecules in aqueous redox flow batteries. By designing zwitterionic naphthalene diimides with quaternary ammonium and sulfonate groups, the researchers achieved solubility and stability in high concentrations (up to 1.3 M). Anion-π interactions with hydrophobic anions disrupted molecular stacking, enabling reversible redox processes without aggregation or rapid self-discharge, as confirmed by spectroscopic methods.

**Key findings**

- Zwitterionic naphthalene diimides with quaternary ammonium and sulfonate groups achieve solubility up to 1.3 M in aqueous redox flow batteries
- Hydrophobic anions like iodide disrupt molecular stacking via anion-π interactions, enabling reversible redox activity
- Reduced species are stabilized through spin pairing and cation-mediated interactions, suppressing aggregation and self-discharge

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes adaptive supramolecular interactions as a design principle for enhancing redox stability and solubility in aqueous redox flow batteries.
