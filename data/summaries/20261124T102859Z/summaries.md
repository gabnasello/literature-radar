## Ultra-Broad-Temperature Mechanoluminescence Enabled by Intrinsic Lattice Disorder.

PMID: 42974761 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42974761/

**Summary**

This study investigates enhancing mechanoluminescence (ML) thermal stability by engineering intrinsic lattice disorder in Sr3Gd2(Ge3O9)2:Mn2+. The researchers created a quasi-continuous trap landscape using diverse local crystal-field environments and dynamically generated defects, enabling a trap-relay process. This approach sustains stable ML across an ultrabroad temperature range (133–433 K), demonstrating potential for extreme-temperature applications without external power. The findings address the limitation of narrow thermal windows in conventional ML materials.

**Key findings**

- Engineered intrinsic lattice disorder in Sr3G:Gd2(Ge3O9)2:Mn2+ enables a quasi-continuous trap landscape
- Trap-relay process sustains stable mechanoluminescence across 133–433 K
- Demonstrated human-machine interaction under simulated extreme-temperature conditions

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes intrinsic lattice disorder as a general strategy to broaden the thermal operating window of mechanoluminescent materials, advancing the design of thermally robust, energy-storing luminescent systems for extreme environments.

## Additives and Relaxation Fluidize Molecular Transport Within Covalent Adaptable Networks.

PMID: 42974849 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42974849/

**Summary**

This study investigated how dynamic covalent bonds in polymer networks influence molecular transport using super-resolution microscopy. The research compared unhydrated networks with and without thiol-thioester dynamic covalent chemistry (DCC), adding catalysts to observe effects on probe diffusion. Results showed that catalyst additives significantly increased mobile fraction and produced more fluid-like diffusivity without macroscopic plasticization. The findings indicate that small additive concentrations can enhance transport by restructuring heterogeneous networks, potentially aiding separation and controlled-release applications.

**Key findings**

- Catalyst additives increased mobile fraction and diffusivity in DCC networks
- Enhanced transport occurred without macroscopic plasticization
- Restructuring of heterogeneous networks, not local bond exchange, drove transport improvements

**Scientific significance**

This work demonstrates that strategic additive incorporation can fluidize molecular transport in covalent adaptable networks through network restructuring, providing insights for stimuli-responsive materials design.

## Crystallinity-Governed Liquid Wettability and Mobility on Solid Surface.

PMID: 42974853 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42974853/

**Summary**

This study investigates how crystalline packing in semicrystalline polymers affects liquid wettability and droplet mobility. By incorporating poly(octadecyl acrylate) into a porous polymer coating, researchers controlled crystallization through melting and cooling pathways. The approach generated reversible hydrophilic, hydrophobic, and amphiphobic states with surface tensions of 22-72 mN m-1, enabling programmable droplet spreading, pinning, and low-friction transport without post-synthetic modifications or external stimuli.

**Key findings**

- Reversible switching between hydrophilic (θW ∼ 85°), hydrophobic (θW ∼ 104°), and amphiphobic slippery states via crystalline packing control
- Programmable droplet spreading, pinning, and low-friction transport across surface tensions of 22-72 mN m-1
- Reconfigurable wettability patterns enabling guided liquid transport through spatially selective wetting

**Scientific significance**

This work demonstrates a crystallinity-driven mechanism for dynamically tuning liquid wettability and mobility, providing a versatile platform for applications requiring reversible, stimulus-free control of liquid behavior on solid surfaces.

This study engineered SIM0278, an IL-2 mutein fused to a human Fc fragment, to enhance regulatory T cell (Treg) selectivity while minimizing conventional T cell activation. The variant demonstrated reduced binding to IL-2Rβγ, preferential STAT5 activation in Tregs, and robust therapeutic efficacy across multiple autoimmune disease models and cynomolgus monkeys. - The mutein showed dose-dependent therapeutic effects in multiple autoimmune disease models
- SIM0278 exhibited prolonged half-life and sustained Treg expansion in cynomolgus monkeys

**Scientific significance**

This study provides a proof-of-concept for engineering IL-2 variants with enhanced Treg selectivity and prolonged half-life, advancing targeted immunomodulation for autoimmune disorders.

**Summary**

The work addresses key constraints in flexible organic imaging systems.

**Key findings**

- PPHJ OPD arrays achieved high responsivity (0.6 A/W at 868 nm)
- Low dark current (1.5 × 10^-8 A/cm² at -1 V) and fast response (480 ns)
- Pixel-shifting in CNN algorithms improved dynamic imaging resolution

**Scientific significance**

This work advances organic photodetector technology for flexible, low-noise near-infrared imaging by integrating optimized material design with machine learning-enhanced imaging systems.

PMID: 42974896 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42974896/

The approach involves magnetron sputtering and heat treatment to create ultra-long helical carbon nanofiber (CNF) 3D-networks on Al. Results show efficient sodium deposition, dendrite suppression, and high electrochemical stability with 99.49% coulombic efficiency over 3000 hours and stable cycling in full cells.

**Key findings**

- NiY-DAC confined in a 20 nm copper layer enables ultra-long helical CNF 3D-networks on Al current collectors
- The modified collector achieves 99.49% coulombic efficiency over 3000 hours at 1.0 mA cm-2
- Full cells demonstrate stable cycling for 1500 cycles at 10 C with low overpotential (3.1 mV)

**Scientific significance**

This work establishes a diatomic catalyst confinement strategy for designing high-conductivity current collectors that enhance sodium deposition kinetics and suppress dendrite formation in sodium metal batteries.

This visible-frequency system enables long-distance nanoscale focusing for applications in noncontact probing, integrated photonics, and optical interconnects.

**Key findings**

- Conversion of surface plasmon polaritons to surface plasmon hyperbolic polaritons in MoOCl2/Au heterostructure
- Optimized MoOCl2 thickness achieved focal length of 2.5λ and focal spot FWHM of λ/5 at 633 nm
- Threefold energy enhancement in the focusing process

**Scientific significance**

## Nanoscale Thermal Imaging of Dislocation-Mediated Heat Transport.

PMID: 42978319 | Journal: Advanced Materials
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42978319/

**Summary**

This study investigates dislocation-mediated heat transport in 5°SrTiO3 low-angle grain boundaries using operando scanning transmission electron microscopy with electron energy-loss spectroscopy thermometry. The approach combines atomic-resolution imaging, vibrational spectroscopy, and nonequilibrium molecular dynamics to map thermal resistance across dislocation arrays. Results show an effective thermal conductance of ∼35.4 MW m-2 K-1, corresponding to a Kapitza length of ∼339 nm. Dislocation cores act as sharp thermal bottlenecks over 2.14-5.17 nm, while inter-core regions exhibit weaker modulation over 8.34-11.57 nm. The findings reveal how atomic-scale core reconstruction creates nanoscale thermal resistance.

**Key findings**

- Dislocation cores function as sharp thermal bottlenecks over 2.14-5.17 nm
- Inter-core regions show weaker thermal modulation over 8.34-11.57 nm
- Atomic-scale core reconstruction generates nanoscale localized thermal resistance

**Scientific significance**

This work provides quantitative descriptors for defect-structure-guided thermal design by elucidating the nanoscale mechanisms of dislocation-mediated thermal resistance.
